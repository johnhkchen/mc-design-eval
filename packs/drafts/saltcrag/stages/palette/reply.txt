**Reading of the style.** Grey rubble base + black tarred board above + black tarred-shingle roof is the storm-grey, pitch-soaked core; limewash, dressed quoins, slate, and glass enter only as the thrift-and-pooled-money accents the story names. The roof band carries the full economy ladder as preserves — tarred shingle (dominant) over thatch and turf — so the village reads mixed, not uniform. The only metal, "a little iron landed by boat," is spent entirely in the decoration: a hung lantern, its chain/hook, and storm bars on the few glazed openings.

One judgment call worth flagging: I seated **limewash as a `base`/preserve accent** rather than a base dominant, because the story frames it as the cottage's "one indulgence" over a fundamentally storm-grey stone village — the grey, not the white, is the dominant read of "saltcrag." If you'd rather the canonical cottage read as whitewashed, swap `wall.field.ground` and `wall.finish.limewash` tiers.
