## Context (self-contained)

Epic **E-39**. This is the story that actually closes the within-family style-blindness. Layer A
(`DiagnoseBuild`, S-164) is one rustic prompt; this story makes its **`expected` knowledge per-style**, and
selects the right suite from a **style the recognition step declares**.

Two coupled needs:
1. **Style identification (prerequisite).** Recognition must emit a `style` on the program so the eval can
   pick the matching Layer-A suite. Today recognition only produces rustic-family programs; the `style`
   field may be implicit in the pack (`rustic`, `saltcrag`) — surface it explicitly.
2. **A second, genuinely different style.** The wrong-style probe showed blindness *within the rustic
   family*; to prove a per-style judge fixes it, there must be ≥2 styles whose `expected` genuinely differ
   (not two rustic reskins). At minimum a non-rustic `expected` profile the Layer-A judge keys on.

## Falsifiable claim (anti-hedge — lead with how it fails)

When Layer A's `expected` is keyed on the declared style, the diagnostic judge will describe a **different
expected roof/wall/opening for a different style** (rustic gable+timber vs the second style's grammar), so a
build is critiqued against *its* style — supplying the within-family gradient the scalar eval lacked.
**It fails if:** recognition can't declare a style distinguishable from the pack id (then style-id is its
own sub-problem); OR the two styles' `expected` come out near-identical because the schema/prompt can only
speak rustic (then the grammar vocabulary is the gate — a generator epic, reported here); OR the per-style
judge improves nothing measurable in S-166's fixture (then the blindness is in the model's reading, not the
prompt — the honest, important result).

## Acceptance Criteria

- [ ] Recognition emits an explicit `style` on the program; the eval suite (Layer A) is **selected by it**,
      with `rustic` and `saltcrag` as distinct registered suites. No per-subject constants.
- [ ] A **second genuinely-different style** is available to the judge as an `expected` profile (a pack
      and/or a Layer-A style descriptor whose roof/wall/opening expectations are not a rustic reskin).
- [ ] `DiagnoseBuild` consumes the declared style and produces style-specific `expected`; fixture tests show
      the *same build* yields *different* `expected` under two styles.
- [ ] `npm test` green; the frozen instrument and its style-blind scalar behaviour are untouched (this is a
      creation-loop change; the scalar instrument's own validation is the roadmap's separate concern).

## Notes

- "Genuinely different" is the bar that matters: if the only honest second style we can build is another
  rustic variant, **say so** — it means real style differentiation needs a grammar-expressing schema first,
  and that becomes the next epic. Don't fake breadth.
