## Context (self-contained)

Epic **E-39**. Today `CritiqueWorkshopRound` returns `WorkshopReply { critique, decision, action }` — one
BAML call does **both** "what's wrong" and "what to do." The user's design splits these: it is messy to
combine the two judgement calls, and the fused call can't be a per-style diagnostic (Layer A) and a unified
router (Layer B) at once.

This story splits them, on the S-163 contract:
- **Layer A — `DiagnoseBuild(concept, render[, style]) → Critique`** (the structured what's-wrong).
- **Layer B — `RouteCritique(Critique) → [{department, idiom, why}]`** (the unified what-to-do, mapping each
  item into the idiom-registry via the S-163 seam).

Wired into the **creation loop** (the autonomy/workshop loop), replacing the fused reply. The frozen scalar
instrument is untouched.

## Falsifiable claim (anti-hedge — lead with how it fails)

Splitting diagnosis (Layer A) from dispatch (Layer B) produces **more correct, more actionable dispatch**
than the fused `WorkshopReply` — the router selects a department/idiom that addresses the worst critique
item at least as often as the fused judge picked the right tool, and the structured expected/present/missing
gives the builder a target the prose `issue` did not. **It fails if:** Layer B just echoes Layer A with no
added correctness (then the split is cost with no benefit — collapse it, report); OR Layer A's structured
output is *less* informative than the old prose (over-structuring loses nuance — report which nuance);
OR the loop regresses because two calls double the latency/variance without raising climb. The bake-off in
S-166 is the referee; this story must leave both paths runnable for it.
