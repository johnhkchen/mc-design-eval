# T-160-01 Review — parametric wall construction from recognition

## Verdict on the falsifiable claim (lead with it)

> *Constructing walls parametrically from the recognized footprint, replacing the voxel walls, will let the
> **cottage climb** where every patch left it flat — and will **not regress** barn/gatehouse.*

**REFUTED on its headline.** The cottage did **not** climb — it **regressed −7** (22→15). The claim's named
alternative outcome is what actually happened, on two fronts at once:

1. **Cottage gate is not wall-watertightness — it's material.** `construct_walls` *did* solidify the cottage
   envelope (the eval's worst defect moved off "structural integrity"), but into a **single uniform brown
   material**, so the cap dropped to **palette** ("no stone base, plaster, or timber contrast") at q=15 —
   *lower* than the holey-wall cap (22). The brush preserves existing per-column zoning but cannot *invent*
   the concept's ground-stone / upper-plaster / timber split; that is the program's `wall.dressing` /
   `wall.infill.upper` roles, which this brush does not consume. So the localized cottage gate is
   **per-storey material assignment**, not envelope holes.
2. **Footprint-alignment is the real sub-problem for sparse shells.** On the barn (and partly the cottage),
   the GLB-voxelized shell is only **20–22% column-filled** and ragged. The brush solidifies the columns that
   *exist* but **morphological close cannot bridge straight-run absent columns** (proven in WG1; dilate/erode
   shadow a flat-boundary gap). So the envelope stays a **colonnade of posts**, not a closed wall (barn +1,
   visually still see-through). Closing these needs the recognized program's **absolute clean rectangle**
   registered to the build frame — Option A in `design.md`, which the ticket explicitly names as "the
   finding." **That is the finding.**

**Where it worked:** the **gatehouse climbed +20** (14→34), with `construct_walls` driving the first +6
(14→20, massing→roof) and a second pass +2 (32→34). Its shell is **32%-filled and ~square**, dense enough for
close+perimeter to close into a **clean solid stone envelope** with a regular window rhythm — eye-confirmed in
`gatehouse-walls-beside.png`. So the move is real *when the shell is dense enough to close*; the cottage/barn
shells are not.

**Batch:** climbed 2/3, mean Δ **+4.7** (cottage −7, barn +1, gatehouse +20). The agent's tool selection was
**correct and non-trivial** throughout — on the cottage it picked `apply_gable_roof` when the cap was the
roof, then **switched to `construct_walls` the moment the cap moved to the walls** (round 1), exactly the
two-tool discrimination the loop is meant to prove.

## Eye verification (ticket requires it for big deltas)

All three deltas verified against `*-walls-beside.png` (judge-free replay of the real trajectories):
- **gatehouse +20 — confirmed.** Solid stone walls, coherent gable, regular windows. Reads as a gatehouse.
- **cottage −7 — confirmed.** Ribbed/colonnade base, monotone brown, oversized overhanging roof. Not a
  Tudor cottage. The score drop is honest, not an over-read.
- **barn +1 — confirmed flat.** Long colonnade of posts under a big roof; straight-run gaps never closed.

The eval did **not** over-read here (unlike the two cases the stage caught earlier) — render and score agree.

## Open concerns / follow-ups (for a human + next ticket)

1. **Cottage palette gate (highest leverage):** teach `constructWalls` (or a sibling) to assign per-storey
   materials from the program's `walls.ground.role` / `walls.upper.role` so a solidified envelope isn't
   monotone. This is the most likely unlock for the cottage. Candidate S-161/S-162.
2. **Sparse-shell footprint registration (Option A):** register the recognized program's **absolute clean
   rectangle** to the build frame and build walls there, instead of regularizing the ragged occupancy. This
   is what the barn needs to close. The ticket named it; the run confirmed it.
3. **Roof/wall footprint coherence:** on cottage/barn the gable roof overhangs well past the wall ring
   (apply_gable_roof derives its footprint from the eave layer, wider than the constructed ring) — amplifies
   the "ribs under a hat" read. Worth aligning the two footprints.
4. **No regression risk introduced to CI:** the brush is additive and pure; the only behavioural change is in
   an `experiments/` harness, not in the measured instrument.

## One-paragraph handoff

The replace-beats-patch move generalizes to walls **only where the shell is dense enough to close into a
clean ring** (gatehouse +20, eye-confirmed). On the sparse cottage/barn shells, regularizing the *occupancy*
footprint can't manufacture the missing columns, and on the cottage, solidifying exposes a **material** cap
the brush doesn't address. Net: a correct, well-tested, minimal brush; an honestly **mixed** measurement
(2/3 climb, target subject regressed); and a sharply localized next gate — **per-storey material from the
program**, and **absolute-footprint registration** — both already named in the ticket as the legitimate
landing place. The renders, not the mean, are the verdict.
