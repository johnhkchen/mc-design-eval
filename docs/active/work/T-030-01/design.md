# Design — T-030-01 (pr-research-desk)

Decide the message strategy: audience, hook, lead framing, threads, trust beats, CTA. Each
decision grounded in Research, with the alternatives I rejected and why.

## Decision 1 — Audience: lead for the AI/ML builder-skeptic; let the realness proof pull the secondary

**Chosen.** Primary = **AI/ML builders & founders** (structured generation, multimodal, agentic
self-judging loops). Secondary = **creative-tools / Minecraft-adjacent**, served by the visual
artifact and the "yep, that's real" beat without bending the script toward them.

**Why.** LinkedIn's distribution rewards a sharp primary. The builder-skeptic is the audience the
*receipts* are built for: a journal that retracts its own overclaims, a categorical judge built
*because* the numeric one saturated, honest null results — these land hardest on people who have
been burned by cherry-picked demos. The secondary audience needs no separate message; the
crude→stunning visual and the 3-D rotation are universally legible and pull them in for free.

**Rejected.** (a) *Lead for the Minecraft crowd* — bigger raw reach but the wrong believers; they'd
enjoy the visual and scroll on, and it would push the script toward spectacle over method, which
forfeits our actual differentiator. (b) *Split the message 50/50* — dilutes the hook; a two-master
brief gives the script desk no clear first sentence.

## Decision 2 — Hook (first 3 seconds): the before/after morph, crude gray box ↔ stunning facade

**Chosen.** Open cold on the **333-block gray box** snapping to the **strong (3/3) Taj facade**
(run 014) — the "ape→man" tease — with one line of tension over it: *"Same model. Same game. What
changed was the method."*

**Why.** It is the most visually violent contrast we own and it is *honest* (both frames are real
artifacts: the capped-brief box and run 014's render). It front-loads the payoff so the skeptic
keeps watching to find out *how*, which is exactly the content we want them to consume. "Same
model" pre-empts the obvious dismissal ("you just used a better model").

**Rejected.** (a) *Open on the score climbing* — too abstract for second 1; numbers earn attention
only after the eye is hooked. (b) *Open on the 3-D rotation* — strongest *proof* but it answers a
question ("is it real?") the viewer hasn't asked yet; it belongs at the payoff, not the hook. (c)
*Open on the concept art* — gorgeous but it's the fakeable thing; opening there undercuts the
honesty thesis before we've earned it.

## Decision 3 — Lead framing: "an engineered quality climb, measured by an instrument we built"

**Chosen lead.** **The measured climb** — "we treated LLM build quality like an instrument problem:
make the same model climb from a gray box to a *strong*-rated facade by adding one technique at a
time, and *measure* every step with a categorical judge." Everything else (velocity, the pivot,
realness, the sculptor vision) hangs off this spine.

**Why.** Of the four candidate angles, this is the one that (a) is uniquely ours, (b) the skeptic
respects, and (c) *contains* the others: the climb is what velocity accelerated, the pivot is a
step *in* the climb, the rotation proves the climb's output is real, and the sculptor is where the
climb is heading. The measurement instrument is the credibility engine — it's why the climb isn't
vibes. It also naturally licenses the trust beats (a real instrument reports null results).

**Rejected as the *lead* (kept as supporting threads/beats):**
- *Autonomous self-judging overnight loops* — a great credibility detail, but leading with "AI
  judges its own work overnight" invites the "marking its own homework" objection before we've
  shown the judge is disciplined. Demote to a trust/method beat (and pair it with: the overnight
  loop that **promoted nothing** — the honesty makes the autonomy believable).
- *The multimodal image-to-3D pivot* — the best *plot twist*, but as a lead it reads as "we
  switched to images," losing the engineered-climb spine. Keep it as the mid-video turn.
- *Velocity ("dozens of builds a day")* — the *engine* of the climb, not the headline; leading
  with speed reads as "we did a lot of runs," not "we made it better, measurably." Second thread.

## Decision 4 — Two threads, woven (matches the script desk's existing 8-beat sketch)

**Chosen.** Thread A = **the measured quality climb** (gray box → design-doc grounding → reference
grounding → one-plane fix → **strong (3/3)**, score overlay rising competent→strong). Thread B =
**velocity & "yep, that's real"** (dozens/day; techniques compound in the journal and *transfer*
across five subjects; the pivot; the explosion of builds; the 3-D rotation realness proof). They
converge on the sculptor vision.

**Why.** The epic and `pr/script/README.md` already commit to two woven threads; the research
confirms both are real and citable (the climb in runs 001→014; the transfer in 019→022). Two
threads give the script a climb (intellectual payoff) *and* a spectacle (emotional payoff) without
either carrying the whole video. Weaving (not sequencing them separately) keeps the 60–90s LinkedIn
runtime tight.

**Rejected.** *Single thread (climb only)* — cleaner but leaves the realness proof and the velocity
story — our two most *shareable* assets — on the floor. *Three+ threads* — over-stuffs the runtime.

## Decision 5 — Trust beats: put the null results *on screen*, as the credibility move

**Chosen.** Three honest beats, woven as quick credibility flashes (not a wallow, per the epic):
1. **The judge exists because the first metric failed** — the numeric rubric saturated at 4 (noise
   ≈ 0.4); we built the categorical judge to resolve what numbers couldn't. *"We didn't trust our
   own metric, so we replaced it."*
2. **We killed our own shortcuts.** Best-of-N: $5.34 + 29 min bought **zero** gain over a $0.76
   one-shot. The detail-stacking pass *regressed* quality. We report the dead ends.
3. **The honest ceiling.** `detail` is still only *competent* — the one dimension that won't climb;
   the overnight loop that **promoted nothing**; the "ceiling" we wrongly declared and then
   retracted. *This* is what makes the strong-rated wins believable.

**Why.** For the skeptic primary, the null results are not a liability to minimize — they are the
*proof of method*. A highlight reel reads as marketing; a climb that openly shows its dead ends
reads as science. This directly serves the epic's Definition of Done ("honest and on-thesis, not a
cherry-picked highlight reel"). Grounding: every beat cites a specific run/journal verdict.

**Rejected.** *Bury the failures / one quick disclaimer* — safer for a hype audience, fatal for a
skeptic one; it would waste the project's single most differentiating asset (its honesty).

## Decision 6 — CTA: lead with "follow the method," offer "discuss," soft-pedal "try it"

**Chosen primary CTA.** **Follow for the method** — "I'm publishing the whole journal — the
principles, the null results, the climb. Follow if you build with LLMs and want the receipts."
Secondary ask: **discuss** — "Which technique would you have bet on? (We were wrong about
best-of-N.)" — an opinion-bait question that invites comments. *Try-it* is soft-pedaled (the
harness isn't a packaged product yet; over-promising a "try it" undercuts the honesty thesis).

**Why.** A skeptic audience converts on *credibility-building follows*, not on a hard product CTA
there's no product to back. "Discuss" exploits LinkedIn's comment-weighted distribution and is
on-brand (we admit we were wrong about best-of-N, inviting others to be wrong too). Pinning the CTA
to the *journal* (a real, linkable artifact) keeps the whole post honest end-to-end.

**Rejected.** (a) *"Try the tool" CTA* — there is no shipped tool; promising one breaks the honesty
contract and the v1-sequencing reality (the sculptor isn't done). (b) *No CTA / pure broadcast* —
forfeits the comment engagement that carries LinkedIn reach.

## Cross-cutting honesty guards (carried into the brief)

- **v1-sequencing caveat is explicit:** real builds are currently rotatable-but-blockier; the
  gorgeous frames are *concepts*. v1 shows concepts as "where it's heading," not as the real build.
  The brief must tell the script desk to caption concept frames as concepts.
- **Concept ≠ real build** is a stated rule, not a footnote — it's *the* honesty hinge of thread B.
- **No invented metrics** — the brief carries a short "receipts table" of only-citable claims so
  S-031 can't accidentally fabricate a number downstream.

## Net strategy (one paragraph, for the handoff)

For the LLM-builder skeptic: hook on the crude→stunning morph ("same model, the method changed"),
lead with the *engineered, measured climb* (categorical judge as the credibility engine), weave the
*velocity + "yep, that's real" rotation* as the second thread, make the *null results the on-screen
proof of method*, land on the *staged-sculptor / Golden Gate vision*, and CTA to **follow the
journal + debate the method**. Every claim cites a run or a journal verdict; nothing is invented.
