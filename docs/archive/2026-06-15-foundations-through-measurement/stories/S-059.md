## Background (self-contained)

Epic **E-18**, the **thin-form fix**. Voxelizing a TRELLIS GLB is the pipeline's decisive form win — except
on **thin/elongated** subjects, the weakest case. **Bow & arrow** voxelizes worst of the 7 (form IoU
**0.473**): thin members (the bowstave, the string, the arrow shaft) are below a voxel thick in places, so
at the global voxel scale they **drop out or stair-step** into disconnected fragments. (The companion
finding, owned by the consolidation: **sword** can't even get a GLB — TRELLIS 500s on the thin blade — so
thin *angular* forms route to text→JSON instead. This story is about the thin subjects that *do* voxelize.)

The fix is a **thin-feature-aware** layer over `voxelizeGlb` (E-16): detect thin members (local thickness
below a threshold, or the mesh's medial axis) and **guarantee they survive** — connectivity (a thin member
becomes a connected chain, never severed/dropped), and finer effective resolution where the geometry is
thin (or a per-subject scale bump so thin members are ≥1 voxel along their thin axis).

## Goal

A thin-feature-preserving voxelization: `voxelizeGlbThin(glb, opts) → occupancy` that detects thin members
and preserves their connectivity / resolves them at finer scale, then builds the glb-voxel as usual. Lift
**bow-and-arrow** (0.473) and any thin parts (e.g. koi fins) — form IoU before/after, and a
**connectivity check** (no dropped thin components). Pure geometry, unit-tested on a synthetic thin mesh.

## Builds on

- E-16 `voxelizeGlb` / `glbVoxelBuild` + the GLB mesh parser (`src/form/` glb-mesh) + the form-IoU metric.
- The `vConcept` scale convention (`src/sculpture.mjs`) for any per-subject scale bump.

## Run-the-test ticket

- `T-059-01` — implement `voxelizeGlbThin` (thin-member detect + connectivity/adaptive-scale); unit-test

  bow-and-arrow (+ koi); record form IoU before/after + a no-dropped-components check.
