## Background (self-contained)

Epic **E-22**. The goal is that the Minecraft build **looks like its references** — the concept image and a
render of the GLB mesh. The old sign-off used proxy metrics (`speckle`, `distinct`, `IoU`, `valueDeltaE`)
that read perfect on a build that looked like static — the metrics weren't cheated, they were the **wrong
target**. This story makes the gate measure the actual goal: visual resemblance to the fixed references.

## Goal

A reusable **resemblance gate** — the acceptance instrument for the whole pipeline — anchored to immutable
references (Rule 1) and producing a human-inspectable artifact (Rule 2):

1. **Triptych.** Render the **concept image**, the **GLB mesh**, and the **Minecraft build** at *one shared
   view* (`BUILDING_VIEW_3Q`) into a single side-by-side image (`concept | mesh | minecraft`). The mesh and
   minecraft renders use the fixed lens from S-075; the concept image is the artist's fixed 3/4 view.
2. **Perceptual resemblance score** (diagnostic, not the verdict): reuse the silhouette IoU for **form**
   agreement + a **palette/material-agreement** score (do the dominant block colors land where the
   concept/mesh put those materials?). Pure/deterministic — built and unit-tested on synthetic images.
3. **Categorical judge** (the verdict): a multimodal judge sees all three images and rules
   `same object | drifted | different object`, with a one-line **named gap** when it's not "same object"
   (which region, which attribute — form / massing / material zoning / palette). Metered `claude -p`.

The references are immutable during the loop (Rule 1); the judge prompt and thresholds are fixed (Rule 5);
the triptych is what a human inspects to confirm or overrule (Rule 2).
