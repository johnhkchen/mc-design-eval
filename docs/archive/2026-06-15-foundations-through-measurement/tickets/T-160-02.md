## Context (self-contained)

Epic **E-38** / Story **S-160**. T-160-01 builds a clean wall *envelope*. This ticket gives that envelope
its **skin as construction, not recolor** — and in doing so swaps the autonomous loop's **blunt patch
tools for fine construction tools** (the design principle behind this whole epic).

**The diagnosis that motivates it** (census of `benchmarks/sculpture/workshop/barn--saltcrag/final-artifact.json`):
the saltcrag pack declares a rich wall vocabulary — rubble `wall.field.ground` (cobblestone), dressed
`wall.dressing.quoin`, `wall.finish.limewash` — but the build placed a **flat cobblestone plane + 140
quoin blocks, no courses, no relief, no limewash banding**, and its metadata shows it **never ran
articulation** (`prompting_method_id: procedural/workshop@1`). The field material applied; the *grammar*
collapsed to a single-block recolor. (The build was also 72% solid `dark_oak_planks` roof-prism — a
separate roof concern, S-150 / the T-160-03 roof note.) So "the materials didn't apply" is really "the
construction-centric skin was never built."

**Why this is replace-beats-patch, again, with the mechanism named.** The loop's current wall move
(`seal_walls`: morphological close + blind fill) and the hardcoded `wallField: "stone_bricks"` are
**cover-up fixes**: they hide a break (fill the hole, paint one block) rather than construct from the
spec. Cover-ups (a) drown the good initial signal — recognized cobblestone overwritten by hardcoded
stone_bricks; clean form buried under fill — and (b) nudge a defect-eval up by *concealing* the defect,
not solving it. The tools we built (`facade-articulation.mjs`, the idiom-registry passes) exist precisely
so the agent can make **fine construction edits** instead of plopping down large simplified fixes.

Reuse (already built — this ticket *wires*, it does not invent):
- `src/view/facade-articulation.mjs` — `quoin`, `pilaster`, `infillPanel`, `eaveOverhang`.
- `src/pack/idiom-registry.mjs` passes — `course.stairs`, `course.slab`, `clinker`, `limewash`,
  `plinth`, `surface.relief`.
- `src/view/opening-dressing.mjs` — `dressOpenings`, `extractApertures`.
- `src/workshop/articulate.mjs` — `articulateArtifact`, `realizeWithArticulation`, `mergePlacements`
  (relief is a POST-realize pass over the envelope; last-writer-wins by position).
- Roles + zones come from the recognition program / pack palette (`<subject>.program.json`,
  `packs/<style>.json`); the eval is `experiments/eval-alignment/defect-eval.mjs`.

## Falsifiable claim (anti-hedge — lead with how it fails)

Driving the **existing** articulation passes from the pack's declared wall roles (quoins at the
envelope's corners, a string-course / clinker on the field, limewash banding, `dressOpenings` around
apertures, `plinth` at the base) — applied as **relief over the T-160-01 envelope**, *replacing* the
`seal_walls`/hardcoded-`wallField` patch path — will make the wall read as **construction** and climb (or
hold) on the defect-eval where the flat recolor plateaued, **without drowning the build** (no solid fill,
no >50%-single-block collapse).

**It fails if:** (a) articulation relief moves the eval *not at all* → then the eval is **blind to
relief/grammar**, and that is the finding (it routes to S-161's contested-middle validation; the render
is the real judge, not the score); (b) the passes **drown the build** like the fill did (e.g. relief
floods the plane) → then we replaced one blunt tool with another, report the collapse; (c) the pack roles
**don't map to zones** in the envelope's frame (corners/storeys/openings not locatable) → then
zone-registration is the real sub-problem (same family as T-160-01's footprint-registration risk). Report
wherever it lands, **with a render beside the concept** — the census counts can't tell relief from a flat
plane, only the eye can.

## Acceptance Criteria

- [ ] A **wall-skin pass** that, given the T-160-01 envelope + the pack palette + the program zones,
      applies the construction-centric idioms **from the declared roles** (field course/clinker, quoin
      dressing at corners, opening dressing, limewash/plinth where declared) as **relief**, via
      `articulateArtifact` — not a single-block fill. Built by *wiring* the existing brushes; no new
      geometry primitives unless a gap is proven and noted.
- [ ] **Replaces** `seal_walls` and the hardcoded `wallField` in `autonomy-loop.mjs` as the wall tool;
      the cover-up/fill path is removed from the loop (the toolset stays minimal — the agent gets *finer*
      tools, not *more* tools).
- [ ] Re-run the autonomous volume batch (cottage, barn, gatehouse + the saltcrag barn skin as the
      witness case); record the per-subject trajectory and **render each final build beside its concept**
      (judge-free).
- [ ] **Recorded honestly:** does the wall now read as construction (quoins/courses/dressing visible in
      the render), or is it still a recolor? If the eval doesn't move, say so and localize whether the
      gap is the skin or the eval's blindness to relief (→ S-161). The render is the evidence.
- [ ] `npm test` green; no per-building constants (drive everything from roles/zones, not subject keys —
      honor the recognize.mjs self-grep discipline); the eval (`defect-eval.mjs`) untouched.

## Notes

- The governing principle (user, 2026-06-15): *we build tools so the autonomous agent can make fine
  construction edits, not plop down large simplified fixes.* A wall pass that ends up flooding a single
  block is a regression to the blunt-fix world even if the eval ticks up — flag it.
- Relief that a single-azimuth or low-res eval can't see is still real to a human at the glance; trust
  the render over the score on this ticket specifically (relief is exactly what a coarse metric misses).
- Roof-prism dominance is **out of scope** here (it's the roof's construction-centric pass, tracked
  separately) — don't try to fix the roof inside the wall skin.
