**What we'd like to plan with you: when and how a town builds a bridge.** Here's our first cut; please change it freely.

1. **Only where a way goes across.** A crossing is built only if the far bank has something the street serves:
   - plots or streets of the town's own plan;
   - the town's farmland (its farm tracks);
   - or a road to somewhere: a caravan route to another town, which we know in the game.

   Otherwise the street **ends at the shore**: a landing, steps down to the water, perhaps a jetty or a fishing stage, and a lamp. The avenue's line past it isn't laid.
2. **Small water is filled, not bridged.** Under some span (2 or 3 cells?) and shallow, a street just crosses on a causeway or a culvert, not a deck: the one-cell bridge goes.
3. **What kind of bridge, by span and kingdom.** Short spans get a single stone arch or a timber beam bridge. Long spans get arches on piers, or a timber trestle. Very long spans (over 40?) aren't built until a later tier: a ferry landing on each bank instead. And the kingdom's dress for each: Lordaeron timber and fieldstone; Dalaran stone arches, its rail, lanterns; Stormwind's stone.
4. **Landings.** Each end gets an abutment, a short ramp or steps where the deck stands above the bank, and the bank faced as a quay for a few blocks either side. No deck should end mid-air or at a cut face.
5. **Where it crosses.** The narrowest crossing near the street's line, square to the water, not wherever the straight avenue happens to meet it. That may bend the avenue to the crossing, as old towns' roads do, and it's what John asked of the farm roads too ("following actual trade routes").
6. **Boats and water.** Clearance over the water (2 now), and an arch or a gap at the channel so a boat can pass under.
