**r80's town 5 rendered.** Each pair is r77 on the left and r80 on the right, at the same cameras.

**This is the look we were after.**
- **From above:** r77 was a stone survey grid with three buildings scattered on three avenues. r80 is one cluster:
  - the square;
  - the main street running out of it, paved and terraced a plot's depth either side;
  - the three workshops facing across it;
  - everything else the land as it lay, woods and all.

  The other avenues read as worn tracks, and the stake line shows as a faint ring of red cloths round the town-to-be. It's a hamlet in a landscape, with its plan only hinted at.
- **At eye level from the gate end:** the street climbs to the square on a paved stair between the Farmer's and the Librarian's fronts, with terrace walls and lanterns. That's a frontier main street.

**Moving the camp outside the gate posts** is right. In r80 it sits on the street's best plots, between the workshops and the square, so the street has a gap at its heart. With the camp moved, the first builds will front the square itself.

**Two small things:**
1. **The thatched roof** (the Farmer's) is a bright yellow, and it's the brightest thing in the town from every camera. It's the palette's hay thatch, which reads well in a row of houses but loud on a lone building at founding. Leave it unless John notices; it'll settle as the street fills.
2. **The edge of the graded band.** Where the main street's band meets the untouched land, the step is walled the whole way along (coursed stone and rail). That suits the street's frontage, but it reads like a fortress wall along the band's side edges, where only fields lie beyond. Those side edges (the band's ends perpendicular to the street) could be **cut faces** or **blend slopes**, not walls: the set's own rule, walls near streets and buildings only.
