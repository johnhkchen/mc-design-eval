Glad town 5 grew in one piece, with the camp gone once the beds stood.

**An opened Lordaeron street: cobbles with a dressed kerb.** The waiting track stays the tan dirt path; the opened street turns grey. That's the clearest change you can see from the air, and it's how an English market town's street reads.

| part | block | rule |
|---|---|---|
| **middle** | a mix by hash of the cell: cobblestone 55%, mossy cobblestone 15%, gravel 20%, andesite 10% | every cell between the kerbs |
| **edges (kerb)** | stone bricks, with a cracked stone brick 1 in 6 by hash | the street's two outer cells, along its length |
| **half step** | cobblestone slab (bottom), mossy cobblestone slab 1 in 4 | on the lower cell where the street climbs a block, as now |
| **where it meets the forum** | the kerb stops; the middle mix runs on into the square | so the square and its streets read as one pavement |

**Notes:**
- **Gravel only on solid ground.** Gravel falls, so on a cell with air under it (a deck, a fill not yet settled) use cobblestone instead. The surface rule can test the block below.
- **A 3-wide avenue** reads as kerb, cobbles, kerb. **A 4-wide lane** is kerb, cobbles, cobbles, kerb.
- **The mix keeps it from looking like a laid floor.** The mossy cobble and gravel read as a worn street; pure cobblestone reads as a castle floor.
- **Hamlet tier can stay a track all the way.** If you want one step between the track and full cobbles, it's **coarse dirt edges round the dirt path**: the track gets worn margins and a defined width, without stone. It could be the main street's look before the first workshop stands, but I wouldn't add it unless John asks. Two states (track, cobbles) are easier to read than three.

**The caravan roads by span:** that's right. One thing for the trestle: put a passing place (a 2-cell wider bay with a lantern) every 16 on spans over 20. Wagons meet on long crossings, and it breaks up a long straight rail.
