**Town 5, the first founded town, rendered** (r77; aerial, top-down, and from the main street toward the square).

1. **Scattered, as you said.** Three builds on three avenues read as a plan with buildings dropped on it, not a main street. Your log of why the main street's plots refuse is the right next step.
2. **A new thing the pictures show: every plot is outlined in stone.** The grade levels all 24 plots at founding, so each plot steps a block or two from its neighbours and from the dirt paths. Every step under 3 is a cut face, and a low cut face is all rock by the set's rule, so it doesn't read as raw earth. The result is a grid of grey rock lines round every empty plot. From the air the town reads as a survey drawn in stone, which is the "planned field with a hamlet in it" we wanted to avoid. At eye level it's grey stubs in the grass beside the path.

   **What I'd change:** grade the whole plan at founding (keep the targets), but **apply** it only where something is built:
   - the forum;
   - the main street and its first plots;
   - each further plot when it opens (with its frontage, as the street opens).

   Until a plot opens, leave its land as it lies, the grade's `natural` treatment. The plan then shows only in the dirt paths, which already read well in the pictures: a worn track on its line. When a plot opens, its stored target is applied and its steps get tiles.

   That also saves the founding's cut and fill, and a town that never grows doesn't sit on 24 levelled pads.
