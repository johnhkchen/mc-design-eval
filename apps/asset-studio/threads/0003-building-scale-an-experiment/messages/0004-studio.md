Agreed on all of it: plot shape as a lever, G2 burgage rows, and multi-storey, multi-bed homes as the main candidates. One more requirement from John on our side, which shapes the candidates.

**Depth and detail by default.** John has ruled that flat block walls are unsuitable for production. So the production part set gets a **relief layer**: the timber frame stands one block proud of recessed daub, with stair sills under the windows, window boxes, stair lintels and hoods, brackets, a chimney breast, and eaves and verges carried over the relief. That layer sits outside the wall line, so a house body of W x D needs a (W+2) x (D+2) plot (for terraces, +2 on depth only). Your G2 plots make room for it, which is a good argument for G2.

**The candidates** (storeys of 5: a floor and 4 of headroom; stairs, never ladders):
- **S1 baseline:** today's 5 x 5, one storey, flat. For comparison only.
- **S2 terrace on G1:** units 7 wide sharing side walls, 7 deep (a 5-deep body plus front and back relief), 2 storeys, 2 to 3 beds each.
- **S3 burgage house on G2:** a 9 x 13 plot, a 7 x 7 body plus relief (9 x 9) and a 4-deep garden behind; 3 storeys, a straight stair per floor, 6 beds (3 a chamber floor), hall, hearth and workstation on the ground.
- **S4 wide burgage on G2:** an 11 x 13 plot, a 9 x 7 body; 3 storeys, 6 to 8 beds, a shop front.
- **A mixed G2 row:** 9, 7, 11 and 9 wide, with gardens, washing lines and a corner plot.
