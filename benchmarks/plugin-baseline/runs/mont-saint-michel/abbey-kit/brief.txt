The Abbey of Mont-Saint-Michel (Benedictine, founded 966; the church 11th century with a Flamboyant Gothic choir of 1446-1521; the Merveille 1211-1228; the spire 1897 by Victor Petitgrand with Emmanuel Frémiet's gilded Saint Michael): the abbey that crowns the granite rock in its bay, built AROUND the summit, its halls stacked down the rock's flanks on three levels. It must read, at a glance, as THIS abbey: one long grey-granite church along the summit (west to east) with steep dark slate roofs; at the crossing, the square tower and the slender, very tall neo-Gothic spire, dark grey with crockets and pinnacles, topped by the GOLDEN archangel with raised sword; the lacy Flamboyant choir at the east end with flying buttresses and pinnacles; on the NORTH side, the Merveille: a sheer three-storey Gothic wall of granite with tall buttresses rising straight off the rock, the cloister on its top (a square garden ringed by a double row of slender colonnettes under a slate roof); on the SOUTH side, the abbots' lodgings (Logis abbatiaux): tall granite ranges with steep slate roofs and tall chimneys stepping down the rock; and at the east foot the fortified entrance (the Châtelet: two round-fronted turrets either side of a steep stair, Belle Chaise behind it).

Scale: 1.4 blocks per metre (a storey is about 4 blocks). Placement code sets the module exactly where the real abbey stands, on the real rock, so every dimension, the GROUND map and the heights below are exact; keep to them. Exterior only: no interiors beyond what shows through windows and the cloister's arcades. No villagers. Nothing may leave its plot or exceed its stated height. Light it for dusk (warm lanterns on the terraces and stairs, floodlit granite, the gilded archangel catching the light) so no mob spawns on a walkable surface.

THE SPIRE: on the crossing tower, a slender octagonal Gothic spire, dark grey (deepslate tiles / polished deepslate / blackstone, with small pinnacles at its base and crockets up its edges), rising to the archangel; THE ARCHANGEL Saint Michael in gold (gold blocks, a little gold-coloured detail) standing on the tip with a raised sword: its top at y 151 (the tallest point of the build: it must be exactly there). THE MERVEILLE along the north edge (the north part of the footprint): a sheer granite wall three storeys high above the falling rock with deep buttresses, narrow pointed windows below and tall ones above; on its roof level the CLOISTER (a square of grass ringed by arcades of slender pale columns (quartz pillars, stone brick walls) under a slate lean-to roof). THE LOGIS ABBATIAUX on the south: tall granite ranges, pointed and square windows, steep slate roofs with dormers and tall stone chimneys, stepping down the south face; the Grand Degré, a steep stair climbing between high walls to the church. THE CHÂTELET and Belle Chaise at the east: the fortified gatehouse: two round-fronted turrets with machicolations and a dark arched entry over a stair, a pointed slate roof behind. Materials: weathered grey granite (stone bricks, cracked and mossy stone bricks low down, andesite / tuff for variation, cobblestone at the rock), dark slate roofs (deepslate tiles and their stairs and slabs), lead-grey spire, a little gold. Where the GROUND map shows bare rock inside the footprint between buildings (courts, terraces), pave it with stone or leave the rock (stone, cobblestone).
