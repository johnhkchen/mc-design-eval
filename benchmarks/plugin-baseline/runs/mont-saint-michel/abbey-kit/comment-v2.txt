Three fixes, keep everything else (the silhouette, the spire and gold archangel, the slate roofs, the cloister are right):
1. One grey everywhere: the abbey's granite is a warm buff-grey with variation. Mix the walls: stone bricks with tuff bricks, polished tuff and some andesite / light gray tones in courses and patches, cracked and mossy stone bricks low down and on the north face, chiseled or smooth stone for string courses, cornices, window surrounds and quoins, so the faces read as dressed granite with depth, not one flat texture.
2. The base reads as one solid wall: the lower part of the plot (below the buildings' floors, toward the plot's edges and the low corners on the GROUND map) is the ROCK the abbey stands on. Make it rock: rough, irregular, stepped and battered surfaces of stone, cobblestone, andesite, tuff and a little gravel with patches of moss and grass, following the GROUND map, so the buildings visibly step up the rock on terraces and the masonry starts above it. Keep the Merveille's north wall rising sheer from the rock, but as masonry on rock, not rock-coloured wall down to y 0.
3. The Merveille and the choir are too flat: give the Merveille's north face its tall projecting buttresses (2 blocks proud, full height, stepped tops) between tall narrow pointed windows on three storeys; give the choir at the east end its flying buttresses (arched struts from outer piers to the clerestory) with crocketed pinnacles (walls / end rods / lanterns on top) and tall pointed traceried windows, so the east end reads as lacy Flamboyant Gothic against the plainer Romanesque nave.
