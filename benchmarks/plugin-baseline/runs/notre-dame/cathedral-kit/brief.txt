Notre-Dame de Paris (1163-1345; Viollet-le-Duc's 19th-century restoration; rebuilt after the 2019 fire and reopened in December 2024): the Gothic cathedral on the Île de la Cité. It must read, at a glance, as THIS cathedral: pale cream limestone (smooth sandstone, sandstone and light stone for the walls and their carved detail), a long nave and choir under a steep lead roof (dark blue-grey: the roof is lead, so deepslate tiles or cyan-grey, not red), transepts with their great ROSE windows north and south; at the WEST end the iconic facade: two massive square towers with flat tops (69 m), the three deep portals at the bottom, the Gallery of Kings, the west rose between the towers, a pierced gallery high up; at the crossing the slender, very tall lead-and-oak SPIRE (dark grey, octagonal, with statues of the apostles climbing its base and a rooster at the tip); the EAST end (chevet): a rounded apse ringed by chapels with the famous long, thin FLYING BUTTRESSES arching from the outer piers up to the choir's upper walls, pinnacles on every pier; gargoyles and pinnacles along the roofline. Proportions: the nave roof ridge about 43 m, the towers 69 m, the spire 96 m; the whole about 128 m long and 48 m wide (40 m at the nave).

Scale: 1.4 blocks per metre (a storey is about 4 blocks).
