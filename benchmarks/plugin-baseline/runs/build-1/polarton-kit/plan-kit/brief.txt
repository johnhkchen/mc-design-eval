The Polarton hillside kit: modules for the expansion area on the hill west of the village, for the same Southern agricultural settlement (a cotton mill village by the Mississippi, about 1880-1930, on a Create-mod server; cotton, tobacco and corn). The streets, levelled pads, retaining walls, driveways and spruce fences are already built (the site picture shows them). The village's lowland is by the stream; the hill climbs west to a plateau, so the lower plots are the working end and the plateau is where people live and gather.

Build it as ONE sample row of modules on a fixed plot grid, so each plot can be cut out as its own module file and set on its pad (turned to face its street):
THE GRID: a dirt-and-gravel lane runs along x at z 0..4 (it stands for the street; the pads' own street is already built); plots are 12 wide (x) and 14 deep (z 5..18); plot i covers x = 12*i .. 12*i+11. Each module stands inside its plot with its front on the lane side (north), its gate at the plot's middle (x = 12*i+5..6). The plot's edge line is already a spruce fence with a 2-wide spruce gate at x 12*i+5..6 and lantern posts at the two front corners: build the same fence, gate and posts (so the module lays over the pad's own exactly), never a different fence. Nothing a module builds may leave its plot. 8 plots in a row (96 wide):
 plot 0 (site plot 1, lowland by the village, faces north): cotton gin house: two storeys, a wagon bay at the front, a loft with a hoist beam and a hay door, a machine room left as a clear bay for the owner's Create gin (a marked shaft opening in the floor and wall)
 plot 1 (site plot 2, plateau, faces west onto the plateau street): one-room schoolhouse with a small bell cupola, a cloakroom porch and a yard bench
 plot 2 (site plot 3, plateau, faces east): hill farmhouse (one and a half storeys, a deep front porch, a kitchen ell at the back)
 plot 3 (site plot 4, lowland by the stream and lane 1, faces south): blacksmith and wheelwright: an open-fronted forge shed with its own chimney, an anvil, a wheel in progress, a cart under a lean-to
 plot 4 (site plot 5, the highest pad, the plateau top, faces east): the settlement church: white clapboard, a steeple seen from the village below, a small graveyard plot with a picket fence
 plot 5 (site plot 6, flat ground south of lane 2, faces north): tobacco curing barn: tall and narrow, vertical boards with vent gaps, hanging tobacco tiers inside, a fire pit or flue at the base
 plot 6 (site plot 7, flat ground south, faces north): corn crib and granary: a slatted crib on piers, a granary with a ramp, sacks and a scale
 plot 7 (site plot 8, plateau edge, faces west): two-storey house for the gin's owner (better-off: a wraparound porch, a bay, a balcony that looks down the hill to the village)
Houses: board-and-batten, clapboard, shingle or brick on stone piers, tin, shingle or slate roofs; woods and roof colours that differ from kits 1 to 3 where you can. Each house sleeps a villager family: 3 to 4 beds, every bed whole, its head against a solid wall (never a window, a door or nothing), two clear blocks above it, reachable from the door; a table, and a stove by the hearth. The working buildings carry their villager job site (the smith's smithing table, the farmer's composter, a cartographer's table in the schoolhouse, a lectern by the church).
THE WAY IN, from the lane to every main door, is held to an accessibility standard: half steps only (bottom slabs: slab, slab, slab), at most 3 in a row then a level landing 2 long, 2 wide or more, level for the last cell before the door; never a stoop of stairs, never a full-block step. Keep the ground floor low: 1 above the yard at most (a 2-cell slab ramp), so the front yard (z 5..8) has room for it.
Stairs inside a player walks to the top without jumping (flight(): the top step flush with the upper floor). Chimneys as the owner wants them: the fire burns at the BOTTOM in a hearth (campfire with hay hidden in the floor), the smoke rises up a 1x1 air flue walled all round to an open mouth 1-2 courses above the roof; the chimney is SET INTO THE WALL (an exterior chimney: the fire just outside the wall line, the wall cell its mouth, the flue outside; or in a partition wall), never standing in a room; the stack is its own masonry running solid through the roof with the roof stopping against it, constant width, no crown; each chimney fits INSIDE the plot (chimney(g, { wall, into, top, ... })). Simple, sturdy, lived in. Lit so no mob spawns.
