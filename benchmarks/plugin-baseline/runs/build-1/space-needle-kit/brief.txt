The Space Needle, Seattle (John Graham & Company with Victor Steinbrueck, 1962, for the World's Fair): a slender tower of
three pairs of steel legs, warm white ("astronaut white"), that splay out at the ground, pinch together at a narrow WAIST
about two thirds of the way up, then flare out again to carry the TOP HOUSE: a flying-saucer disc ringed by a slatted
HALO of radiating ribs, the glazed restaurant and observation deck inside it, a small domed roof (gold-orange "galaxy gold"
trim on the roof's band), and a thin spire with a beacon. Between the legs the open-lattice core carries the glass
elevators (one gold elevator car visible). At 30 m up, the SkyLine level: a small hexagonal platform among the legs. It must
read, at a glance, as THIS tower: the hourglass legs and the halo-ringed saucer on top.
