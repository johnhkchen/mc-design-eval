A grand Southern plantation house on the Tennessee/Mississippi River, Greek Revival antebellum style: a white two-storey mansion with tall columns across the front carrying a full-width second-storey porch (a double gallery), a low hipped roof with dormers and chimneys, tall shuttered windows, a wide central hall. A commission appraised at about a million: the finest, most complete build yet.
Inside: 8 bedrooms (one of them the master suite, the largest, with its own bath and the best view onto the upper porch), a kitchen, a dining room, and a living room with a vaulted ceiling (open two storeys high, or a coved vault into the roof). The kitchen and the living room are connected, open to each other. A grand stair in the entrance hall; bathrooms near the bedrooms; the upper porch reached from the upstairs hall.
The grounds: a straight path (a drive) leading into the property from the edge of the site to the front steps, lined with an allee of large oaks; auxiliary buildings (a carriage house, a guest cottage, a garden pavilion or gazebo, a greenhouse) set around the house; landscaping: lawns, a formal garden with hedged beds and a fountain, flowering trees, a pond or a river frontage at the back. Keep the whole estate within about 96 x 96 blocks.
