The San Francisco Ferry Building (A. Page Brown, 1898) at the foot of Market Street: a long, low two-storey Beaux-Arts
terminal of pale grey-cream sandstone on the Embarcadero, its land front an unbroken ARCADE of round arches on the ground
floor with a row of arched windows above, a cornice and a low parapet; in the middle a grand entrance pavilion of three tall
arches, and rising from it the CLOCK TOWER modelled on the Giralda of Seville: a tall square shaft with a big round clock face
on each side, then stacked open belfry stages with columns and arches, a lantern, a small dome and a flagpole. Behind the
front a long grey roof over the nave (the marketplace), a skylight running its whole length; on the bay side grey gabled halls
with huge round-arched windows. It must read, at a glance, as THIS building: the white clock tower over the long arcade.
