The Palace of Fine Arts, San Francisco (Bernard Maybeck, 1915, for the Panama-Pacific International Exposition; rebuilt in
concrete 1964-67): a romantic Roman ruin by a lagoon. An open octagonal rotunda with a great salmon-ochre dome, flanked by two
curving peristyles (colonnades) of paired Corinthian columns carrying an entablature, topped by tall open planter boxes with a
draped weeping figure leaning on each corner, and behind them the long curved exhibition hall. Everything is the colour of weathered
tinted plaster: warm sandstone and buff, the dome a deeper salmon-ochre, the column shafts slightly lighter, shadows in the deep coffers.
It must read, at a glance, as THIS building: the domed rotunda on its ring of giant arches, and the colonnade marching away behind it.

Scale: 1.4 blocks per metre (a column 1.2 m across is 2 blocks; a 3.6 m storey about 5). The modules are for a public catalog of
buildings for a Minecraft 1.21.1 server: use only blocks that exist in 1.21.1 (no pale oak, nothing from 1.21.4+). Placement code
will set them on the real site from city data, so every module must keep exactly to its plot and its stated size.

Build it as ONE row of four modules on fixed plots along x, each standing at y 0 on a flat 1-block ground of smooth stone
(paving), its front facing north (low z):
 plot 0, x 0..63, z 0..63 (64 x 64), up to y 70: THE ROTUNDA. An open octagon about 58 blocks across: on each of the eight faces a
   giant round arch (about 26 blocks tall) between massive piers, each pier faced with clustered Corinthian columns; over the arches
   an entablature and a tall attic (the drum) carrying a relief frieze band; on the drum the dome, about 48 blocks across, peak near
   y 68, salmon-ochre, with ribs, and a coffered underside seen from inside. The inside is one open, walkable space (the public walks
   through it), floored in paving. No windows or doors: it is open to the air through its arches.
 plot 1, x 64..79, z 0..23 (16 x 24), up to y 32: A PERISTYLE BAY, the repeating unit of the colonnade. Two rows of paired Corinthian
   columns (shafts 2 x 2, bases and capitals proud) running along x, about 20 blocks to the top of the capitals, a full entablature
   (architrave, frieze, cornice) on top tying both rows, and on top of that one open planter box (about 8 blocks tall, a hollow
   box with leaves/vines spilling out) with a draped weeping figure suggested at each top corner (blocks, not entities). The bay
   must TILE: placed end to end along x it continues seamlessly (its x ends are where the next bay's begin; half a column spacing
   at each end, no end walls). A walkway of paving between the rows.
 plot 2, x 80..95, z 0..23 (16 x 24), up to y 32: A PERISTYLE END, where a colonnade arm stops: the same columns, entablature and
   planter box as plot 1 at its west end (so it joins a bay), and at its east end a closing pier: a solid square pier the depth of
   the colonnade with a niche, carrying the entablature round the corner.
 plot 3, x 96..111, z 0..23 (16 x 24), up to y 34: AN EXHIBITION HALL BAY, the repeating unit of the long curved hall behind the
   colonnade: a tall solid wall (about 22 blocks to the cornice, then a parapet) facing north, with a large round-arched recessed
   entrance or a tall blind arch in the middle, a frieze band and cornice, pilasters at the bay ends; behind the wall a simple
   pitched roof down to the back (south). It must TILE along x like plot 1.

No furniture, beds, rooms or villagers: this is a monument. Light it so no mob spawns (lanterns hidden in the coffers and on the
pier faces, glowstone in the dome's underside). Nothing may leave its plot.
