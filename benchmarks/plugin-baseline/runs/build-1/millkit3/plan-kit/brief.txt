A third modular housing kit for the same Southern agricultural settlement (a cotton mill village by the Mississippi, about 1880-1930, on a Create-mod server; cotton, tobacco and corn; the plantation house and two mill-village rows are nearby). It complements kit 1 (shotgun house, saddlebag duplex, hall-and-parlour cottage, kitchen garden, chicken coop, well yard, company store) and kit 2 (dogtrot, camelback shotgun, I-house, pyramid cottage, boarding house, smokehouse work yard, pecan and bee yard, 1920s bungalow): new house types, a mix of one and two storeys and of plain and better-off houses, and two shared yards. Build it as ONE sample row of modules on the SAME fixed plot grid, so each plot can be cut out as its own module file and mixed with kits 1 and 2 in any layout.
THE GRID (exact, identical to kits 1 and 2): a dirt-and-gravel lane runs along x at z 0..4 (with a lantern post every 12 blocks at the lane's edge, at the same x as the other kits); plots are 12 wide (x) and 14 deep (z 5..18); plot i covers x = 12*i .. 12*i+11. Each module stands inside its plot with its front on the lane side (north), its door or gate at the plot's middle (x = 12*i+5..6), and the plot's two side columns (x = 12*i and 12*i+11) kept as a low fence or hedge line or open path so neighbours join cleanly. Nothing a module builds may leave its plot. 8 plots in a row (96 wide):
 plot 0: double shotgun (two narrow shotgun units side by side under one front-gable roof, a shared middle wall, two front doors, a shared porch)
 plot 1: Creole cottage (side-gabled, steep roof sweeping over a full-width front galerie on slim posts, two pairs of French doors, Mississippi River style)
 plot 2: American Foursquare (two storeys, square, hipped roof with a front dormer, full-width front porch, 1910s)
 plot 3: Queen Anne cottage for the mill superintendent (the best house on the street: wraparound porch with turned posts, a corner tower or bay, patterned shingles, two storeys)
 plot 4: gable-front-and-wing farmhouse (an L of a front gable and a side wing, one and a half storeys, porch in the angle of the L)
 plot 5: mule barn and wagon shed (stalls with hay, a hay loft, a wagon under a lean-to, a fenced paddock; villager job sites that fit: a composter, a smithing table, a leather worker's cauldron)
 plot 6: bake-oven and cistern yard (a shared brick bread oven with its own fire and flue, a rain cistern of barrels or a small water tank on legs, a picnic pavilion with tables)
 plot 7: two-storey company duplex (brick, side by side, two front doors, a two-storey porch, 1920s)
Houses: board-and-batten, clapboard, shingle or brick on brick or stone piers, tin, shingle or slate roofs; use woods and roof colours that differ from kits 1 and 2 where you can so a mixed street varies; porches, real windows. Each house sleeps a villager family: 3 to 4 beds per house (duplex units 2 to 3 each), every bed whole, its head against a solid wall (never a window, a door or nothing), two clear blocks above it, reachable from the door; a table, and a stove by the hearth. Stairs a player walks to the top without jumping (flight(): the top step flush with the upper floor). Chimneys as the owner wants them: the fire burns at the BOTTOM in a hearth (campfire with hay hidden in the floor), the smoke rises up a 1x1 air flue walled all round to an open mouth 1-2 courses above the roof; the chimney is SET INTO THE WALL (an exterior chimney: the fire just outside the wall line, the wall cell its mouth, the flue outside; or in a partition wall), never standing in a room; the stack is its own masonry running solid through the roof with the roof stopping against it, constant width, no crown; plan each house so its chimney fits INSIDE the plot (an exterior side chimney needs the house wall at least 2 cells in from the plot's side column; otherwise put it on the back wall or in a partition) (chimney(g, { wall, into, top, ... })). Simple, sturdy, lived in; each house a little different. Lit so no mob spawns.
