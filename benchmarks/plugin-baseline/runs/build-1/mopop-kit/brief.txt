The Museum of Pop Culture (MoPOP; Frank Gehry, 2000) at Seattle Center: a sculptural cluster of swooping, crumpled, billowing metal-skinned forms, each its own colour (polished silver, gold, deep purple/magenta, red, sky blue), like a smashed electric guitar, with the Seattle Center Monorail running straight through the building between the forms on its concrete beam. It must read, at a glance, as THIS building: the clashing coloured blobs and the monorail passing through.

Scale: 1.4 blocks per metre. For a public catalog for a Minecraft 1.21.1 server: only blocks that exist in 1.21.1 (no pale oak, nothing 1.21.4+). These are the real neighbours of the Space Needle at Seattle Center; placement code sets each module on its real footprint exactly as it stands (no turning), so build each building IN ITS SITE ORIENTATION: north is low z, east is high x, as in the footprint map below, and keep its walls to the map (# = the building, . = open ground inside the plot, which may get paving, steps or planting). Each module stands at y 0 on a flat 1-block ground of smooth stone. Use the photographs only as inspiration for character and colour. No villagers. Light every building so no mob spawns. Nothing may leave its plot or exceed its stated height.

ONE module, plot 0, x 0..101, z 0..152 (102 x 153), up to y 46: MOPOP, the whole building on its footprint (map below). Shape the forms with stairs and slabs so their surfaces curve and billow (no flat boxes): a big silver form, a gold one, a purple one (purple/magenta terracotta or concrete), a red one, a blue one, each a separate rounded mass, some with deep folds, overlapping. Entrances with glass at ground level between the forms. The monorail: a pair of grey concrete beams about 2 apart, on T-shaped pylons, running north-south straight through the plot at about y 9..10 (the track passes between/under the forms, through a gap), with a short white-and-red monorail train (about 18 long) on it inside the building. A paved plaza with steps around the building in the open parts of the plot.
