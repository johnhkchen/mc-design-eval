Make it read the way the real castle does, in this order of importance:
1. VERTICALITY. It reads wide and squat; it must read tall and narrow. Make the stair turret (the slender round tower beside the Palas) the unmistakable high point: thinner, with an octagonal belfry stage near the top and a needle spire, clearly 15+ blocks above every other roof. Make the Palas taller and narrower-looking (5 storeys, steeper roof, a higher ridge), and put the whole castle on a much taller sheer cliff: deepen the rock crag so it drops away steeply on the front and sides (extend the build downward for this, don't squash the castle to fit).
2. PALAS FACADES. Replace the flat grid of dark rectangles with Romanesque-revival windows: paired and triple round-arched windows with thin white column mullions, grouped in vertical bays between pilaster strips, each storey's windows different from the next. Give the courtyard-facing front a two-storey open arcaded balcony (the Singers' Hall loggia), and make the north-face balcony bold and visible.
3. ROOFS AND CAPS. Gold finials on every spire and cone; vary the slate a little between the big roofs and the turret caps so they aren't one flat black; keep the cones slender and pointed.
4. INTERIORS (last, as time allows). Inside the Palas: the Throne Hall, two storeys tall, blue walls with gold, an apse at one end with a raised gold throne platform and a large chandelier; above it the Singers' Hall, a long hall with a coffered wooden ceiling and the loggia doors. Give the other storeys floors so windows look into rooms.
Keep everything else as it is: the gatehouse, the gate towers, the courtyards, the Knights' House and Bower, the palette.
