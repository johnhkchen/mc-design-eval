 plot 0, x 0..56, z 0..20 (57 x 21), up to y 27: THE SMITHSONIAN CASTLE — A picturesque Norman Romanesque revival castle of deep red-maroon Seneca sandstone (red terracotta, polished granite and red nether brick details; never bright red), long along x with its main front facing NORTH to the Mall (low z). NINE TOWERS of different heights and shapes give its unmistakable jagged silhouette: the tallest, the slender north-centre tower (the Flag Tower, about 19 blocks above the base) with a steep pyramidal spire; a big square tower just east of centre; a pair of tall octagonal towers on the south front; smaller turrets and pinnacles at the corners and wing ends; every tower roof a steep dark grey slate pyramid or cone (deepslate tiles stairs) with small finials. The main block three storeys, rows of tall round-arched windows (dark glass, warm light at dusk), corbel tables under the eaves, steep dark slate roofs; lower wings at each end (the west wing with a chapel-like range of tall arched windows), a central north entrance with a round-arched porch. Lit warmly at dusk.
 plot 1, x 61..102, z 0..41 (42 x 42), up to y 22: THE ARTS AND INDUSTRIES BUILDING (1881) — A colourful Victorian exhibition hall in red brick with polychrome bands (bricks, red/orange/yellow/blue terracotta bands and patterns): a Greek cross plan inside a square, a central octagonal rotunda with a raised clerestory and a lantern, four tall gabled naves running out to the four sides each ending in a big gabled entrance pavilion with a large arched window and flanking towers; low corner pavilions with small towers; grey slate roofs with clerestory windows along every nave; the north entrance pavilion faces the Mall.
 plot 2, x 107..136, z 0..25 (30 x 26), up to y 16: THE FREER GALLERY OF ART (1923) — A low, two-storey Italian Renaissance palazzo in pale pink-grey granite (white terracotta, smooth stone, polished diorite trim): a square block around a central open courtyard (with a small fountain and trees), its north front on the Mall with an arched loggia entrance of three round arches, small square windows, a heavy cornice and a low hipped grey roof.
 plot 3, x 141..169, z 0..30 (29 x 31), up to y 19: THE HIRSHHORN MUSEUM (1974) — A hollow cylinder of rough pinkish-beige precast concrete (white terracotta / smooth sandstone mixed with light grey concrete) about 29 across and 6-7 blocks tall, RAISED one storey above the ground on four massive curved piers so its plaza runs under it; a round open courtyard in the middle with a round fountain; the outer wall almost blank, but one long horizontal window slit (a balcony band of dark glass) runs all round the inner courtyard face on the top floor. A circular paved plaza around it with low walls and a few sculptures (dark blocks).
