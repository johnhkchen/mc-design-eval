 plot 0, x 0..100, z 0..39 (101 x 40), up to y 34: THE NATIONAL GALLERY OF ART, WEST BUILDING — A very long, low, symmetrical neoclassical temple of pale rose-pink Tennessee marble (white terracotta, with smooth quartz and calcite for the columns, cornices and trim; the pink graduating slightly paler toward the top), running along x. At its centre a LOW DOME on a drum over a rotunda (shallow, Pantheon-like, dark grey-green lead/copper: weathered copper or dark prismarine/green-grey, with a round oculus lantern), reaching about 22 blocks. On the south front (high z, facing the Mall) a grand Ionic portico of eight white columns under a plain pediment at the top of wide steps; a matching portico on the north front (Constitution Avenue). The long wings either side of the centre are almost windowless walls of pink marble divided by tall shallow pilasters, a plain frieze and cornice, ending in projecting end pavilions; the roof is flat with long rows of glass skylights (glass, light grey frames) over the galleries. Warm floodlights at the portico at dusk.
 plot 1, x 105..153, z 0..33 (49 x 34), up to y 30: THE NATIONAL GALLERY OF ART, EAST BUILDING — I. M. Pei's abstract geometric building of the SAME pale pink marble as the West Building (white terracotta), almost windowless: a trapezoid divided into two triangles, an isosceles triangle on the west (facing the West Building across the plaza, its entrance a deep dark recessed slot) and a smaller right triangle on the east; at the corners sharp tall prismatic towers rising above the main roof (parallelogram-shaped, flat-topped, about 21 blocks); the famous razor-sharp acute angle at the south-west corner (a knife edge); between the two triangles a huge glass atrium roof of triangular skylights in a space frame (glass panes in a light grey grid) that glows at dusk. Large plain planar walls, no ornament, crisp edges, a few horizontal dark window bands only.
