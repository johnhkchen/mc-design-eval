30 Rockefeller Plaza (the Comcast Building; the RCA Building of 1933, the GE Building 1988-2015), Raymond Hood and the Associated Architects: the 70-storey Art Deco slab at the heart of Rockefeller Center and the hero of the render. It must read, at a glance, as THIS tower: a thin, sheer, pale limestone slab, narrow end to the plaza (east), broad flanks north and south that step back in a cascade of setbacks, every face ribbed by continuous vertical limestone piers with dark recessed window strips between them, so it reads as a bundle of vertical lines soaring to a flat crown.

Scale: 0.9 blocks per metre (a storey is about 3.5 blocks). THE TOWER is the tall slab in the east part of the footprint (its narrow EAST face on Rockefeller Plaza, at the footprint's high-x edge, faces the sunken plaza): the east and west faces rise sheer; the long NORTH and SOUTH flanks step back in many narrow setbacks (each a ledge 1-3 blocks deep, crowned with stair/slab copings), so from the air the slab narrows in tiers toward its top. Walls: pale buff Indiana limestone (smooth_sandstone, sandstone, end_stone_bricks or white_terracotta in a consistent light buff) as continuous vertical PIERS 1 block wide, proud of the wall, with 1-block recessed vertical window strips between them (dark: gray or black stained glass panes, with dark gray_terracotta or polished deepslate spandrels between storeys), so every face is fluted with vertical lines top to bottom; the corners are solid limestone. THE CROWN: the top three tiers are the Top of the Rock observation decks: open terraces with glass-pane parapets, the topmost a narrow flat roof with a low mechanical penthouse and a slim mast; around the crown a band of big illuminated red letters (the GE-era red-lit sign: red concrete or red stained glass over glowstone/shroomlight) on the north and south faces near the top, and the crown floodlit warm white (light blocks/shroomlights hidden in the setback ledges so the upper faces glow). THE ENTRANCE: at the base of the east face on Rockefeller Plaza, the great entrance: three tall portals under Lee Lawrie's WISDOM relief, a large polychrome-and-gold Art Deco panel over the doors (a bearded figure with dividers over gold rays and a glass block screen: gold blocks, yellow and light blue terracotta, glass) with the inscription band; lit lobby glass below. THE SIXTH AVENUE WING (the west, lower part of the footprint, about 16 storeys, as the roof map shows) is the same limestone and vertical-pier language, its flat roofs with low parapets and a few planted roof gardens, entrances and lit shopfronts on Sixth Avenue (the west face) and on 49th and 50th Streets (the south and north faces).
