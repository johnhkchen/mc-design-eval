The twin spires read right; keep them, the plots and every footprint exactly. But the hero camera looks DOWN on the cathedral from high over Fifth Avenue, and from above it reads as an open shell:
1. The NAVE, the CHOIR and the LADY CHAPEL have no roof: you can see down into the walls. Roof the whole cross like the transept: one continuous steep pitched roof (stairs at 45 degrees or steeper, deepslate tiles / cobbled deepslate, the same dark slate as the transept, with the thin copper-green ridge) running the full length from the back of the west towers to the east end, over the clerestory walls, at the same ridge height as the transept so the crossing reads as a cross from the air; a lower hipped/apsidal roof over the Lady Chapel at the far east end; the side aisles get lower lean-to slate roofs below the clerestory windows, with the flying buttresses arching over them. No openings into the interior from above.
2. The WEST FRONT on Fifth Avenue (the low-x face, between the two towers) needs the great ROSE WINDOW: a round wheel window about 9 across of coloured stained glass in white marble tracery, lit from within, centred above the portals and below the central gable; under it three deep pointed portals with layered arches and dark bronze doors (the middle one tallest), lamps either side and broad steps down to the sidewalk.
3. Give the pinnacles along the nave and transepts sharper tops (end rods or pointed dripstone over a wall) so the roofline bristles from the air.
Keep the two residences as they are.
