St Patrick's Cathedral (James Renwick Jr., 1878, spires 1888), the Roman Catholic cathedral of New York, on Fifth Avenue across from Rockefeller Center between 50th and 51st Streets: a white marble Gothic Revival cathedral among the towers. It must read, at a glance, as THIS cathedral, especially from the air: a pale white-grey marble cross-shaped church, its WEST FRONT on Fifth Avenue with TWIN OPENWORK SPIRES and the great ROSE WINDOW between them, a long steep nave roof with pinnacles and flying buttresses, transepts, and the Lady Chapel at the east end.

 plot 0, x 0..104, z 0..41 (105 x 42), up to y 96: THE CATHEDRAL — A Latin cross: the long NAVE along x from the west front (Fifth Avenue, LOW x, the footprint's west edge) to the east, the TRANSEPTS crossing it north-south about the middle (their ends on 50th Street to the south, high z, and 51st Street to the north, low z), the choir and apse beyond, and at the far EAST end the lower LADY CHAPEL (follow the FOOTPRINT and ROOF HEIGHT maps). Walls of white-grey marble (calcite, diorite, polished diorite, smooth quartz, white concrete, in a soft pale grey-white mix, not pure white) with tall pointed-arch stained-glass windows between buttresses (glass: purple, blue, red, yellow stained glass panes in pointed lancets, LIT FROM WITHIN so they glow at dusk), pinnacles (end rods, walls, pointed dripstone) topping every buttress, FLYING BUTTRESSES arching over the side aisles to the clerestory, a pierced parapet. ROOFS: a steep pitched roof over nave, transepts and choir (stairs at 45 degrees or steeper, dark grey-green slate: deepslate tiles, cobbled deepslate or dark prismarine, with a thin copper-green ridge), a small fleche at the crossing. THE WEST FRONT on Fifth Avenue: two square towers rising into TWIN OPENWORK GOTHIC SPIRES, each tower about 9 x 9 blocks, its belfry with tall louvred openings, then an octagonal pierced spire tapering to a point and a cross at about y 90 (white marble with lace-like tracery: walls, fences, slabs alternating with open gaps), crocketed edges; between the towers the tall central gable over the great bronze doors (three deep pointed portals with layered arches), and above them the great ROSE WINDOW (a round wheel window about 9 across of coloured glass in stone tracery, lit from within). Wide steps down to the Fifth Avenue sidewalk, lamps. NOTE on the maps: the lidar missed much of the cathedral's dark roof (cells reading 1-2 inside the footprint are gaps, not courtyards): the nave, transepts, choir and Lady Chapel are all roofed; the 33-41 cells show the real eaves and ridge heights.
