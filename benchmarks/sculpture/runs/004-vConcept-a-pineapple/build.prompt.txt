## Finalized design document
# Freestanding Sculpture — Design Document: Pineapple

## 1. Read of the subject
A pineapple is fundamentally **two stacked masses**: a fat, barrel-to-ovoid golden **fruit body** crowned by a burst of stiff, radiating **green leaves**. Two features make it instantly nameable from any angle: the body's **diamond cross-hatch lattice** (the eyes) and the spiky **leaf crown** flaring up and outward like a green flame. Get those two right and no viewer mistakes it.

## 2. Form & proportion (in the round)
Split the 32-block height roughly **5 : 18 : 9** (base : body : crown). The body is an **ovoid of revolution** — circular in plan so it reads identically from front, side, and 3/4 — widest (~16 blocks Ø) at ~40% height, tapering to ~10 Ø at the shoulders. The crown is **6–8 leaf blades** of stepped height (tallest ~12, outer ~6) splaying in radial symmetry, so the silhouette is a textured oval below and a jagged star above from every direction. A **flattened 2-block disc base** (slightly wider than the body's bottom) gives a stable, non-tippy footing.

## 3. Color palette (warm analogous harmony)
Pineapples live in the **yellow-orange-green** analogous band — warm, ripe, appetizing.
- **Dominant:** Yellow Terracotta (muted gold body — earthier than concrete, reads as fruit not toy).
- **Supporting:** Orange Terracotta (the diamond "eyes," giving ripeness and depth) and Green Concrete (the crown — a saturated jolt that pops against the warm body via the one cool note).
- **Accent (sparing):** Lime Terracotta — a few highlight tips on the tallest leaves only.

## 4. Surface & motifs
**Motif A — diamond lattice:** Orange Terracotta blocks set in a staggered, every-other diagonal grid across the body, the unmistakable pineapple skin at block scale. **Motif B — leaf ridging:** each crown blade is a 1-block-thick vertical fin, stepped at the tip, never merged into a solid cap.

## 5. Block-budget plan
Longest dimension = **32 (height)**. Base 2 · body 18 (≤16-block footprint) · crown 12. Plan footprint stays within **16×16**, compact and turntable-ready.

## Scale (honor it)
- Target ~32 blocks along the LONGEST dimension. Bounding box up to ~32(x) × 32(y)
  × 32(z); build a compact, solid object that fills that envelope without sprawling.
- Use `fill`/`box` for the major masses and `line` for struts/edges so you can afford solid
  volume; reserve `voxel` (with block `state` for stairs/slabs) for shaping curves, facets,
  and surface detail. Approximate curves/round forms with stepped stairs+slabs, not flat panels.

## Realize the document with craft
- Build the essential masses and the recognizable features the document names; honor its
  proportions and its dominant/supporting/accent palette hierarchy.
- Keep the object SOLID and connected — no detached floating parts unless the subject truly has
  them (and then bridge them so they are supported).
