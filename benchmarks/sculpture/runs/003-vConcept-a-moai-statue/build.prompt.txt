## Finalized design document
# Moai Sculpture — Design Document

## 1. Read of the subject
A moai is a **monolithic ancestor-figure**: one continuous column of volcanic stone that resolves into a colossal human head-and-torso. It is NOT a free human shape — the body is vestigial, the head dominant. Three features make it instantly nameable: the **massive wedge brow** shadowing the eyes, the **long straight nose dropping into a heavy projecting jaw**, and the **thin downturned lips**. Get those and it reads "moai" from across the room. Arms are flat reliefs pinned to the sides, hands meeting low on the belly — present, but they must never break the columnar silhouette.

## 2. Form & proportion (in the round)
One stacked column, slight forward lean (the iconic skyward tilt). From bottom up:
- **Base/plinth** ~4 blocks — a wider grey footing so the heavy head can't read as top-heavy or float.
- **Torso block** ~12 blocks — a rounded rectangular slab, narrowing slightly toward shoulders.
- **Head** ~16 blocks — the prize, ~½ the total height (real moai run ~3/8; exaggerate for recognition).

Front silhouette: a vertical loaf crowned by a blocky head. **Side** is the money angle — the nose/brow/jaw step OUT in a staircase, chin overhanging. 3/4 fuses both: profile relief plus frontal face. Footprint stays compact (~12×10) so it spins cleanly on a turntable.

## 3. Color palette (apply color theory)
**Monochromatic stone harmony** — a moai is one quarried mass, so unity beats contrast. 
- **Dominant:** Gray Concrete (clean weathered tuff). 
- **Supporting:** Andesite + Stone for mottled, carved variation across the body. 
- **Accent (sparing):** **Red Sandstone pukao (topknot)** — the single warm note, historically correct, drawing the eye to the crown.

## 4. Surface & motifs
Two block-scale motifs: **stepped facial planes** (brow→nose→lip→chin carved as receding ledges, the recurring rhythm of the whole piece) and a **2-block-deep eye-socket recess** under the brow — carved by *omitting* front blocks, not burying them. Cheeks and torso stay smooth to let the face dominate.

## 5. Block-budget plan
32 longest edge (height): plinth 4 · torso 12 · head 16. Head spends ~6 on the projecting face-stack, leaving a 2–3 block pukao cap. Width ≤12, depth ≤10 — a dense, readable monolith.

## Scale (honor it)
- Target ~32 blocks along the LONGEST dimension. Bounding box up to ~32(x) × 32(y)
  × 32(z); build a compact, solid object that fills that envelope without sprawling.
- Use `fill`/`box` for the major masses and `line` for struts/edges so you can afford solid
  volume; reserve `voxel` (with block `state` for stairs/slabs) for shaping curves, facets,
  and surface detail. Approximate curves/round forms with stepped stairs+slabs, not flat panels.

## Realize the document with craft

- Keep the object SOLID and connected — no detached floating parts unless the subject truly has
  them (and then bridge them so they are supported).
