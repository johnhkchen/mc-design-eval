# Temple of the Cobalt Dawn — Facade Design Document

## 1. Lore & Setting
A Mughal-descended sun-and-sky temple of an invented river dynasty — a paradise-gate where pilgrims greet the dawn. The reference's serene, perfectly mirrored calm becomes sacred symmetry: a face built to be photographed head-on at sunrise, when its cobalt crown catches first light.

## 2. Aesthetic & Architectural Logic
Read from the reference: a cubic block with **chamfered corners**, a deep double-height central **iwan** (great pointed-arch portal) framed by a rectangular **pishtaq** band, flanked by two stacked registers of recessed niche-arches. A bulbous **onion dome** on a tall drum crowns it, echoed by four corner **chhatri** kiosks and flanking spires. The logic works because every void is framed and every mass is mirrored — relief reads cleanly from straight on, exactly what a facade-only build needs.

## 3. Color Palette — Warm-Complementary, Cobalt-Crowned
The reference is white marble; I refuse the white wall. I borrow Mughal **red sandstone** instead and strike it against a cold crown — a confident **complementary** harmony.
- **Smooth red sandstone** — dominant terracotta body
- **Cut red sandstone** — supporting relief banding & string-courses
- **Lapis block** — the onion dome, drum chevrons, iwan vault (cold complement, the crown)
- **Gold block** — finial, pishtaq calligraphy frame, voussoir ring, chhatri caps (metallic accent)
- **Amethyst block** — pietra-dura floral medallions in spandrels (jewel accent)

Terracotta vs. cobalt makes the dome blaze; gold and amethyst are the inlay sparkle.

## 4. Motifs & Ornament
Adapted from the reference: the **pishtaq** rectangular frame in gold calligraphic banding around the iwan; **spandrel medallions** rendered as amethyst-and-gold rosettes; the **lotus-chevron** dome base in alternating lapis/gold; floral **pietra-dura** strips along every string-course; gold **guldasta** corner finials.

## 5. Architectural Features & Proportion
- **Base:** raised cut-sandstone plinth, full width.
- **Body:** cube ~36 wide × ~36 to cornice, chamfered corners; iwan ~⅓ width, rising ⅔ of cube height; flanking niches in two registers, each ~half the iwan width.
- **Entablature:** gold-banded cornice with amethyst dentils.
- **Crown:** lapis drum (~⅓ cube height) + onion dome belly **overhanging** the drum, gold finial — dome diameter ≈ iwan width, total crown ≈ ½ cube height.
- **Openings:** deep recessed iwan (relief to z-depth), framed by engaged pilasters; four chhatri at roof corners.

Ratios: cube ≈ 1:1; iwan 1:3 of width; crown 1:2 of body.

## A facade is ONE connected plane (critical)
Borrow the reference's RHYTHM, not its 3-D standalone parts. A real building's corner towers or
minarets are freestanding in the round; in a flat elevation they must read as ENGAGED — bonded to
the wall and projecting forward as relief, NEVER as detached pillars with sky between them. Do not
pull columns, towers, or framing off the body; every mass stays connected into a single facade.
