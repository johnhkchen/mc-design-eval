## Finalized design document
# The Gate of the Dawn-Lotus — Sun-Court Temple Facade

## 1. Lore & Setting
The Sun-Court of Vermaar, a river-delta tradition of tile-makers and astronomers, raised this gate-temple to greet the first light of the monsoon's end. Like the reference, a mausoleum-grade monument of perfect symmetry, it is a *ceremonial face*: pilgrims approach along a reflecting canal and read the whole elevation at once. Each color band records a season of the pilgrim year.

## 2. Aesthetic & Architectural Logic
From the reference I take: strict bilateral symmetry; a tall central pointed-arch *iwan* (recessed portal) flanked by two stacked arched niches per side; a single dominant onion dome with a finial; four slender tapering minarets that frame the block and make a stepped silhouette (low wings → tall center → dome → tall towers); and a raised plinth. Symmetry plus a recessed center gives instant focus and a deep shadow read at block scale.

## 3. Color Palette — Split-Complementary with Gold
- **Dominant — Terracotta Glazed (`orange_terracotta`)** for wall fields: warm, earthy, carries the mass.
- **Supporting — Turquoise (`warped_planks` / `cyan_terracotta`)** for dome, niche interiors and portal recess: cool contrast to the orange, as in glazed-tile traditions.
- **Supporting — Indigo (`blue_terracotta`)** for the plinth band and minaret rings, anchoring the base.
- **Accent — Gold (`gold_block`, `yellow_concrete`)** for finial, string-course, and arch outlines.

Orange against turquoise/indigo is a near-complementary pair; gold bridges them and draws the eye upward.

## 4. Motifs & Ornament
- **Pointed-arch niche**, repeated at three scales (portal, wing niches, tiny dome-drum windows) for rhythm.
- **Lotus-pinnacle**: small gold-capped chhatri spikes (end rods on gold) at the corners, echoing the reference's rooflines.
- **Calligraphic frame**: a gold-and-indigo rectangular border (`alfa` frame) around the portal, built from alternating stripes and stair blocks.
- **Banded minarets**: gold rings every 6 blocks.

## 5. Features & Proportion (facade ≈ 61 wide × 46 tall, relief depth 5)
- **Plinth:** 4 tall, full width, indigo, with stair-block cornice stepping out 1 block.
- **Main block:** 33 wide × 22 tall (ratio ~3:2), terracotta.
- **Central iwan:** 9 wide × 17 tall, pointed arch (stair-capped), recessed 4 deep, turquoise interior with a dark door (`spruce_door`) and a small arched window above.
- **Flanking niches:** two stacked per side, 5 wide × 7 tall, recessed 2 deep.
- **Parapet/entablature:** gold string-course plus a crenellated terracotta/stair band at y+22.
- **Dome:** 15 diameter, turquoise, sitting on an 8-tall drum with 4 arched windows; bulbous profile widest at ⅓ height, then tapering to a gold finial 7 tall. Total height ≈ 46 (dome ≈ ⅓ of the whole).
- **Minarets:** 4 total, 3×3 footprint, 34 tall (outer pair) and 22 (inner pair), each capped with a small turquoise dome and gold spire.
- **Small corner domes:** two 7-wide turquoise domes flanking the main dome.

## Scale & RELIEF — use it (this is judged)
- Width up to ~56 (X), height up to ~48 (Y) including the crown. Build big.
- DEEP relief: up to ~24 blocks of depth into −Z, and genuinely use it. Columns/buttresses
  project several blocks PROUD of the wall; the central portal RECESSES several blocks deep;
  cornices, string courses, and the crown step forward and back across MULTIPLE Z-layers. A
  near-flat screen only 4–6 deep is a failure.
- CROWN proportion: the crowning element (pediment / parapet / attic / cresting) must span the
  facade's full width and have real height — a deliberate culmination of the body, never a
  small cap perched on a wide base.
