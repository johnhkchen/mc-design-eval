# Temple of the Kindled Dawn — Facade Design Document

## 1. Lore & setting
A high, cold desert plateau where nights are brutal and the first sun is salvation. The **Ashvane** people built this temple to **Saeled, the Kindler** — the deity who relights the world each dawn. Worship is a sunrise rite: pilgrims face the facade head-on as light floods it, so the *front elevation is the entire liturgy*. The temple is cut from and clad in local iron-rich stone, the only abundant material on the plateau.

## 2. Aesthetic & architectural logic
A **massive, battered (inward-sloping) wall** broken by deep engaged piers — heavy and earth-rooted because the plateau quakes and wind scours, so mass and a low center of gravity are structural necessities, not style. Relief is **deep**: piers and recessed niches throw long shadows at dawn, dramatizing the light the cult venerates. The silhouette is a stepped, rising **ziggurat-gable** crowned by a sun-disc — vertical pull toward the sky-god, grounded by a broad plinth.

## 3. Color palette (accented analogous)
Warm analogous body — red→orange→ochre — reading as sun-warmed stone, with one cool **complementary** accent reserved for the sacred threshold (the moment cold night yields to warm dawn).
- **Dominant** — Red Sandstone (warm ochre field, ~65%)
- **Supporting** — Smooth Red Sandstone (crisp pier/cornice edges); Orange Terracotta (warm relief, friezes)
- **Accent (sparing)** — Gold Block (the sun-disc and finials only); Light Blue Glazed Terracotta (threshold/inner-niche glint only)

Restraint: warm hues dominate; gold and blue together are <8% of the face, concentrated at the entrance and crown.

## 4. Motifs & ornament
- **The rayed sun-disc** — crowning finial, echoed as small medallions on pier capitals.
- **Stepped chevrons** ("rising light") — banded frieze across the entablature and flanking the door.
- **Vertical fluting** — incised into each pier, drawing the eye upward.

## 5. Features & proportion
- **Base:** wide stepped plinth, 3 courses, full width.
- **Supports:** 4 engaged battered piers dividing the face into 3 bays; central bay widest.
- **Entablature:** chevron frieze + projecting cornice.
- **Crown:** stepped gable with central gold sun-disc.
- **Entrance:** tall recessed central portal, blue-glinted reveal; flanking bays hold deep shadow niches.
- **Proportion:** facade **5 wide : 8 tall** (≈golden); plinth : wall : crown = **1 : 3 : 1.5**; central bay = **2×** side bays.

## Scale & relief (keep this — it works)
- Width up to ~48 (X), height up to ~40 (Y) incl. crown. Build big.
- DEEP relief up to ~16 into −Z, genuinely used: columns/buttresses project several blocks
  proud; the central portal recesses several blocks; cornices/crown step across Z-layers.
- CROWN spans the facade's full width with real height — a culmination, not a small cap.

## Surface detail & ornament (the FOCUS this run — judged hardest)
- NO large flat single-material fields. Break every wall plane with coursing, banding,
  recessed panels, pilaster strips, or contrasting trim.
- Articulate EVERY transition with a moulding/string course: base→shaft→capital, wall→
  entablature→cornice→crown. Put a dentil or stepped course under the main cornice.
- Ornament the facade: fluting/reeding on columns and pilasters, a repeating frieze motif,
  medallions/rosettes/inlay panels, coffered or panelled recesses, a carved tympanum.
- Micro-texture: use 2–3 related blocks within each material family (e.g. smooth / cut /
  chiseled, or slabs+stairs as relief) so surfaces have grain, not one flat block.
- Frame every opening with a layered moulding (several stair/slab courses) and a deep reveal.
- Work lighting in as ornament (lanterns/sea-lanterns in niches, glow accents in the frieze).
- Use voxel block `state` (stairs/slabs facing/half) liberally for all the above; avoid uniform
  45° slopes. Use `fill`/`box`/`line` for masses and runs so you can afford the detail.
