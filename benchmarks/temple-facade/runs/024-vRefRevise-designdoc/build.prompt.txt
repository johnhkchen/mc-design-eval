## Finalized design document
# Temple of the Lapis Meridian — Facade Design Document

## 1. Lore & setting
A Mughal-descended sun-temple of an invented dynasty that mineralized devotion: where the reference is a *tomb* of mourning rendered in pale marble, this is a *living shrine* to a midday god, so its stone is dyed the blue of zenith sky veined with gold. Same imperial grammar, opposite emotional key — grief made into triumph.

## 2. Aesthetic & architectural logic
Read from the reference: rigorous bilateral symmetry, a tripartite front — central **pīshtāq** (the great recessed pointed-arch portal) flanked by two stacked register of smaller arched niches — capped by a bulbous onion dome on a high drum, with four engaged corner minarets pinning the cube. It works because the deep central iwan reads as a shadowed gateway pulling the eye in, while the framed flanking panels keep the wall from going blank. I keep all of it and translate only the surface color.

## 3. Color palette (color theory)
**Split-complementary**, blue-anchored:
- **lapis_block** — dominant field (the sky-stone body, ~65%)
- **smooth_quartz** — supporting (plinth, arch rings, minaret shafts — the original marble, demoted to trim)
- **gold_block** — supporting accent (string-courses, pilasters, dome, finials — the divine metal)
- **red_nether_bricks** — sparing crimson accent (~5%, rosettes/inlay)

Blue against gold-and-crimson is the proven Mughal-inlay chord; quartz cools it so it never turns garish.

## 4. Motifs & ornament
From the pietra dura inlay and spandrel work: **gold ogee frames** around every niche, **crimson quatrefoil rosettes** set in recessed lapis panels, **continuous gold string-courses** echoing the reference's horizontal banding, and **chattri kiosks** (quartz columns, gold finials) flanking the dome base.

## 5. Architectural features & proportion
- **Base:** quartz plinth, y0–6, gold cap course.
- **Body:** lapis cube, central iwan recessed **5 blocks** deep with a quartz pointed-arch ring; two flanking niche-bays per side.
- **Supports:** engaged 4-wide lapis minarets bonded to corners, quartz pinstripes, gold balcony rings.
- **Entablature:** three gold string-courses (≈ every 6–8 rows) so no tall field rises unbroken.
- **Crown:** onion dome whose **belly is wider than its drum** (or it reads as a pyramid), gold finial spire.
- **Proportion:** iwan height ≈ ⅔ facade; dome ≈ ⅓; minarets frame at full height — the reference's ~1:1.6 portal-to-body ratio held throughout.

## Orientation (photographed head-on)
Facade FACES +Z, in the X–Y plane (X = width, Y = height, y = 0 ground). Front face at the
highest Z; relief recedes into −Z. Model only the front and its relief.

## Scale & RELIEF — use it (this is judged)
- Width up to ~56 (X), height up to ~48 (Y) including the crown. Build big.
- DEEP relief: up to ~24 blocks of depth into −Z, and genuinely use it. Columns/buttresses
  project several blocks PROUD of the wall; the central portal RECESSES several blocks deep;
  cornices, string courses, and the crown step forward and back across MULTIPLE Z-layers. A
  near-flat screen only 4–6 deep is a failure.
- CROWN proportion: the crowning element (pediment / parapet / attic / cresting) must span the
  facade's full width and have real height — a deliberate culmination of the body, never a
  small cap perched on a wide base.
