# Design Document: The Temple of the Ninefold Lamp

## 1. Lore & Setting

A memorial-temple built in the 1690s on a harbor city's cliff, commissioned by a merchant-widow for her husband, a lamp-keeper of the sea guilds. The tradition holds that a lamp kept burning on the terrace guides ships home. Glazed tile stands in for the lantern: at dusk the inlay catches the light the way the reference's marble catches the sky. The temple is a memorial and a navigation mark, so its face must read from far out at sea.

## 2. Aesthetic & Architectural Logic

The reference's language is **a single dominant mass, symmetrical, with slender verticals that lift it**. The dome crowns a block, minarets frame it, and recessed arches give depth. Its surface detail is inlay (pietra dura), not sculpture, so ornament reads as pattern at distance and as craft up close. I keep that logic and take the reference's form, not its color. Its marble-white body becomes a warm terracotta body, because the brief demands color and a white wall would fail it.

## 3. Color Palette

Five Minecraft 1.20.1 blocks. **Harmony: complementary.** Warm orange carries the body, cool blue-cyan carries the ornament and crown, and gold adds a warm accent.

| Role | Block | Share | Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| Dominant | `orange_terracotta` | ~60% | Walls, plinth, drum |
| Supporting | `cyan_glazed_terracotta` | ~15% | Inlay bands, arch spandrels, medallions (rotated for pattern) |
| Crown | `lapis_block` | ~10% | Main dome, chhatri domes |
| Trim | `smooth_quartz` | ~10% | Cornices, window frames, minaret rings (never a full wall) |
| Accent | `gold_block` | <3% | Finials, lotus caps, lamp at the peak |

Orange against cyan-blue sits at the widest hue distance, so the ornament pops against the body.

## 4. Motifs & Ornament

- **Pointed pishtaq arch:** the central recessed entrance, framed by a tall rectangular panel.
- **Chhatri kiosks:** four domed pavilions at the corners of the roofline, echoing the reference's flanking domes.
- **Minaret rings:** stacked balconies on each slender tower.
- **Glazed medallions:** cyan lozenges set in the orange wall beside each arch.
- **Lattice tracery:** window openings patterned with quartz-and-cyan grids.

## 5. Architectural Features & Proportion

Ratios read from the reference, taken from the photograph's proportions (not the building's documented dimensions):

- **Overall width : central block width ≈ 2 : 1.** Facade 41 blocks wide, central block 21.
- **Minaret height ≈ 1.7 × central wall height.** Minarets 3×3 at base, about 17 blocks above the plinth. The wall is 10 blocks.
- **Dome diameter ≈ 0.44 × central block width** (9 blocks), dome height ≈ 0.8 × diameter (about 7 blocks).
- **Pishtaq width ≈ 0.23 × central block width** (5 blocks), arch height ≈ 0.7 × wall height (7 blocks).

**Base:** a 2-block plinth in orange terracotta, capped with a quartz course.
**Supports:** orange wall piers with cyan spandrels between arches.
**Entablature:** a quartz cornice with a cyan inlay band beneath.
**Crown:** a lapis dome on a quartz drum, with a gold finial and lamp at the apex.
**Entrance:** a 5-wide pishtaq, recessed two blocks, with the cyan medallion above.

## A facade is ONE connected plane (critical)
Borrow the reference's RHYTHM, not its 3-D standalone parts. A real building's corner towers or
minarets are freestanding in the round; in a flat elevation they must read as ENGAGED — bonded to
the wall and projecting forward as relief, NEVER as detached pillars with sky between them. Do not
pull columns, towers, or framing off the body; every mass stays connected into a single facade.
