# Temple of the Vermilion Dawn

## 1. Lore & setting
A sun-temple of an invented Indo-Persian river-cult, raised where the delta meets the desert. Like its white forebear it is a *threshold* — a serene, perfectly symmetric face built to be read head-on across still water — but consecrated to first light rather than mourning: a monument to dawn, not to the dead.

## 2. Aesthetic & architectural logic
The reference reads as one chamfered cube crowned by a bulbous onion dome on a constricted drum, its front opened by a single towering **pishtaq** — a rectangular frame enclosing a deep pointed-arch iwan — flanked by two tiers of smaller recessed arches, the corners answered by domed chhatris and edge minarets. It works because every element is nested and self-similar: the great arch echoes the small ones, the dome echoes the kiosks. I keep that recursive, mirror-symmetric grammar; it makes a flat face read as deep and inevitable.

## 3. Color palette (color theory)
**Complementary, warm-vs-cool.** The brief demands color where the marble is white, so I invert it:
- **Red Sandstone** — dominant warm body (~60%).
- **Dark Prismarine** — the onion dome and string-courses, a teal complement (~25%).
- **Gold Block** — finial, spandrel medallions, crowning accent (~8%).
- **Lapis Lazuli Block** — inlaid panel-fields, cool depth (~5%).
- **Polished Blackstone** — the dark calligraphic band framing the iwan (~2%).

Orange against teal, lit by gold: confident, unmistakably not-white.

## 4. Motifs & ornament
Adapted from the reference: the dark inlay border tracing the pishtaq (blackstone), pietra-dura medallions in the spandrels (gold-and-lapis), stacked niche-pairs on the chamfered corners, and a lotus finial. No flat fields — every panel is framed once by a recessed course.

## 5. Architectural features & proportion
Base: a one-block plinth. Body: a chamfered cube, **width ≈ height-to-cornice (1:1)**. Pishtaq: **two-thirds** the body height, recessed 3 blocks. Drum + dome ≈ the body's upper third, the dome's belly **overhanging the drum** so it never collapses into a pyramid. Engaged minaret-pylons at the facade's outer edges and chhatris at each shoulder hold the silhouette; guldasta pinnacles cap the frame corners.
