# Temple of the Lapis Meridian

## 1. Lore & setting
A sun-and-water temple of an invented Indo-Persian dynasty, raised over a sacred reflecting tank. Where the reference is a tomb of mourning rendered in pale marble, this is a temple of *daybreak* — the same disciplined Mughal grammar turned jubilant, built to catch the first light and throw it back in color.

## 2. Aesthetic & architectural logic
The reference reads as a single cubic mass with **chamfered corners**, crowned by a bulbous onion dome on a high drum, attended by four corner chattris and framed by four detached minarets. Its power is *centered symmetry*: one dominant pointed-arch iwan governs a facade of stacked, recessed niches. I keep this hierarchy intact — one great arch, subordinate twins — because it makes a flat front read as deep, monumental architecture rather than wall.

## 3. Color palette (color theory)
A **complementary harmony, amber ↔ azure**, gilded:
- **Red Sandstone** (smooth + cut) — *dominant* warm-terracotta mass.
- **Blue Glazed Terracotta** — *supporting* azure for iwan spandrels and inlay panels; the direct complement makes the body glow.
- **Gold Block** — *accent*: dome cap, finial, and calligraphic arch-border.
- **Dark Prismarine** — small jade note in the inlay, echoing pietra dura jadework.

This refuses the reference's white: the marble becomes vermilion stone, the inlay becomes saturated lapis and jade — bold, not bleached.

## 4. Motifs & ornament
- **Pishtaq calligraphy band** — gold border framing the central arch (adapts the reference's inscription bands).
- **Pietra-dura panels** — azure-and-jade recessed medallions in the spandrels.
- **Guldasta pinnacles** — slender gold-tipped finials at the corners of the iwan frame.
- **Recessed niche grid** — paired stacked arches flanking the center, carved by *omitting* front material for true relief depth.

## 5. Architectural features & proportion
- **Base:** a raised plinth, ~1/8 of facade height, in cut red sandstone.
- **Mass:** chamfered cube; iwan width ≈ **½** the facade width.
- **Crowning:** gold onion dome whose belly **overhangs its drum** (so it reads as a dome, not a pyramid); height of dome ≈ cube height, holding the famous near-1:1 square envelope.
- **Flanking chattris:** four small domed kiosks at the mass corners.
- **Entrance:** single tall pointed-arch iwan, deeply recessed, gold-framed, over a dark threshold.

## A facade is ONE connected plane (critical)
Borrow the reference's RHYTHM, not its 3-D standalone parts. A real building's corner towers or
minarets are freestanding in the round; in a flat elevation they must read as ENGAGED — bonded to
the wall and projecting forward as relief, NEVER as detached pillars with sky between them. Do not
pull columns, towers, or framing off the body; every mass stays connected into a single facade.
