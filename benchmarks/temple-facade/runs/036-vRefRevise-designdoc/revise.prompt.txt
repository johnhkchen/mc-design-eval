# The Lantern Temple of Saltmere: Front Elevation

## 1. Lore & setting
Saltmere's pilots lost eleven boats in one storm. Their guild raised this temple to the Keeper of Lights. The tall towers hold dusk lamps that guide boats home, and the Lantern at the crown never goes dark. Serene, symmetrical, devotional: like the reference, it keeps the lost in sight by keeping light on.

## 2. Aesthetic & architectural logic
The reference rests on strict bilateral symmetry, one dominant dome, slim towers framing the mass, a plinth lifting the whole, and inlay rather than deep carving, so the dome is the single big moment. Blocks cannot show marble's soft shading, so the restraint becomes depth: a projecting frame, a recessed portal, a stepped dome. Form comes from the reference; color comes from the brief.

## 3. Color palette: split-complementary
Orange dominates. Its split complements (teal and ultramarine) carry the cool dome and inlay. Gold, analogous to orange, binds the scheme.

- **Inlay, 15%:** `minecraft:blue_glazed_terracotta`: scroll band, rosettes, plinth panels, minaret rings
- **Accent, 5%:** `minecraft:gold_block`: finial, drum ring

## A facade is ONE connected plane (critical)
Borrow the reference's RHYTHM, not its 3-D standalone parts. A real building's corner towers or
minarets are freestanding in the round; in a flat elevation they must read as ENGAGED — bonded to
the wall and projecting forward as relief, NEVER as detached pillars with sky between them. Do not
pull columns, towers, or framing off the body; every mass stays connected into a single facade.
