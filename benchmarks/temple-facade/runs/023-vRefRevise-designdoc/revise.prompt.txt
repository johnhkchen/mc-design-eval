# Temple of the Lapis Crown

## 1. Lore & setting
A river-shrine to the Goddess of Still Water, raised by a jewel-trading dynasty whose artisans inlaid devotion in stone. Where the reference is a mausoleum of mourning rendered in pale marble, this is a living temple of *celebration* — the same Mughal grammar of symmetry and serenity, but blazing with the lapis and gold the merchants grew rich upon.

## 2. Aesthetic & architectural logic
The reference reads as **perfect bilateral symmetry built from one device repeated at three scales**: the great *pishtaq* (recessed pointed-arch portal) framed by a rectangular *iwan*, echoed by smaller paired arches in the flanking bays, crowned by a swelling bulbous dome on a high drum. Chamfered corners turn the cube into an octagonal silhouette so it never reads flat. This works because hierarchy is total — one dominant axis, every element subordinate to it. I keep that discipline and recolor it.

## 3. Color palette (color theory)
A **complementary harmony with a neutral pivot** — warm gold against cool blue, calmed by cream:
- **Dominant — Lapis Lazuli Block:** deep ultramarine field, the temple's body.
- **Supporting — Smooth Quartz:** cream arch-frames and the dome, structuring the blue like marble seams.
- **Accent — Gold Block:** finial, dome ribs, inlay lines (the spark opposite the blue).
- **Plinth — Red Nether Brick:** a grounding terracotta base.

Blue/gold complementaries vibrate; quartz keeps it readable, not garish.

## 4. Motifs & ornament
From the reference: the **pointed-arch spandrel**, **dentil string-courses**, **corner finials (guldastas)**, and **inlaid floral cartouches**. Adapted: gold-block chevrons trace each arch; quartz-stair dentils run the entablature; lapis spandrels carry a gold rosette; the chamfered corners rise into slender quartz finial-pillars capped in gold.

## 5. Architectural features & proportion
- **Base:** red-nether-brick plinth, 1/6 of total height.
- **Body:** lapis cube, chamfered corners; central pishtaq recessed **3 blocks deep** for true relief shadow.
- **Bays:** tripartite — wide central portal flanked by two stacked quartz-framed arches.
- **Entablature:** gold-and-quartz dentil band.
- **Crown:** bulbous quartz dome on a tall drum, gold ribs converging to a gold finial — dome belly **overhangs the drum** so it reads as an onion, not a pyramid.
- **Key ratios:** portal height ≈ **0.6** of facade; dome ≈ **0.4 of width**; width:height ≈ **3:2**.

## A facade is ONE connected plane (critical)
Borrow the reference's RHYTHM, not its 3-D standalone parts. A real building's corner towers or
minarets are freestanding in the round; in a flat elevation they must read as ENGAGED — bonded to
the wall and projecting forward as relief, NEVER as detached pillars with sky between them. Do not
pull columns, towers, or framing off the body; every mass stays connected into a single facade.
