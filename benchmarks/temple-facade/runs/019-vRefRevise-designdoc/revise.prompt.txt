# Temple of the Vermilion Eaves

## 1. Lore & setting
A mountain monastery of the **Cinnabar Way**, a pilgrim cult that lacquered its halls vermilion to ward decay and honor the sunrise. The reference — Hōryū-ji's Kondō and pagoda — gives a 7th-century East-Asian timber grammar: a religion expressed through roofs, not walls. This facade is the Kondō's front, the one hall a pilgrim meets head-on.

## 2. Aesthetic & architectural logic
What I read: **deep, double-tiered hipped-gable roofs** with enormous eave overhangs carried on visible bracket clusters; a post-and-beam wall of rhythmic bays; a stone podium with a central stair. The logic is *shelter as ornament* — the eaves' shadow-line is the building's strongest gesture. I keep that, stacking two roofs so silhouette reads at distance, and fold the pagoda's diminishing tiered spire into the crown rather than copying it as a detached tower.

## 3. Color palette (complementary, red–green axis)
The reference is white plaster, dark wood, gray tile — information, not mandate. I translate it to lacquered temple color:
- **Red Concrete** — *dominant*: columns, beams, wall posts (vermilion).
- **Prismarine Bricks** — *supporting/complement*: glazed celadon-jade roofs; the textured face breaks flat fields and casts micro-shadow.
- **Calcite** — *neutral ground*: plaster infill panels between the red posts.
- **Gold Block** — *accent, sparing*: the sōrin finial, bracket caps, ridge ends.

Red against jade is a true complementary pair; gold (a warm near-split of red) lifts the crown; calcite keeps it from screaming. Bold, never monochrome.

## 4. Motifs & ornament
- **Bracket clusters (tokyō)** stepped out under each eave as corbel relief — gold-capped.
- **Rhythmic bays**: red posts framing recessed calcite panels (recess by *omission* of the front course, not by burying a block).
- **Ridge finials** and a sōrin-style stacked crown.
- **Balustrade** banding the upper tier.

## 5. Architectural features & proportion
- **Base:** calcite/deepslate podium, ~1/6 of total height, with a centered ascending stair.
- **Body:** two bays of red post-and-panel, lower tier wider.
- **Eaves:** roof depth ≈ wall height (1:1) — deep overhang is the whole point.
- **Crown:** double jade roof, the upper ~0.75× the lower, topped by the gold sōrin spire.
- **Symmetry:** strict bilateral; central bay widest (~1.4× side bays); facade roughly **3 wide : 2 tall**.

## A facade is ONE connected plane (critical)
Borrow the reference's RHYTHM, not its 3-D standalone parts. A real building's corner towers or
minarets are freestanding in the round; in a flat elevation they must read as ENGAGED — bonded to
the wall and projecting forward as relief, NEVER as detached pillars with sky between them. Do not
pull columns, towers, or framing off the body; every mass stays connected into a single facade.
