## Finalized design document
# Temple of the Stormward Eye — Front Elevation Design Document

## 1. Lore & Setting
The **Vautri**, a highland people of a wind-scoured volcanic plateau, carve sanctuaries into ochre tuff cliffs to honor **Ashk, the Stormward Eye** — guardian deity who watches the horizon and breaks the killing storms before they reach the terraces below. The temple faces the prevailing weather: to look upon its face is to stand where the god stands, between the people and the sky. The climate is dry, fierce-sunned, lashed by seasonal gales.

## 2. Aesthetic & Architectural Logic
The language is **massive, battered (inward-leaning) cliff-cut relief** — a rock-hewn facade, not a freestanding wall, because the soft tuff is quarried in place, not stacked. Heavy battered piers and a deep recessed portal resist wind-load and shade the entrance from the punishing sun; the silhouette steps upward to a single crowning **eye-aperture** that catches first light. Squat proportions and deep shadow-relief read as permanence — a god who does not move. Ornament is incised and inlaid, since carving suits the stone better than carpentry.

## 3. Color Palette (analogous earth + complementary accent)
Dominant **warm ochre** (the living cliff) grounds the whole; supporting **deep terracotta** and **burnt umber** give the relief its hierarchy of shadow — an **analogous** earth harmony, drawn straight from the volcanic landscape so the temple reads as *of* its place. A single **lapis-blue** accent — the complementary jolt — marks the sacred eye and lintel band, the sky-god's color set against his earthen house.

- Ochre → **smooth red sandstone**
- Terracotta → **terracotta block**
- Burnt umber → **granite**
- Lapis accent → **blue glazed terracotta** (sparingly: the eye, the lintel)

## 4. Motifs & Ornament
- **The Eye** — radiating lidded oculus, crowning the facade and repeated small above each pier.
- **Storm-chevron** — stacked zigzag bands along the entablature and pier capitals (lightning).
- **Terrace-step fret** — incised step pattern at the base, echoing the people's farmed slopes.

## 5. Features & Proportion
A broad three-step **base** (full width); **four battered piers** dividing three bays; a deep central **portal** (twice the side bays' width) under a heavy lapis-banded **lintel**; a chevron **cornice**; flanking shadowed **niches** for offerings; crowning **eye-aperture** centered. Governing ratio **3:2** (height:width), piers on a **1:2:1** bay rhythm.

## Orientation (photographed head-on)
Facade FACES +Z, in the X–Y plane (X = width, Y = height, y = 0 ground). Front face at the
highest Z; relief recedes into −Z. Model only the front and its relief.

## Scale & RELIEF — use it (this is judged)
- Width up to ~48 (X), height up to ~40 (Y) including the crown. Build big.
- DEEP relief: up to ~16 blocks of depth into −Z, and genuinely use it. Columns/buttresses
  project several blocks PROUD of the wall; the central portal RECESSES several blocks deep;
  cornices, string courses, and the crown step forward and back across MULTIPLE Z-layers. A
  near-flat screen only 4–6 deep is a failure.
- CROWN proportion: the crowning element (pediment / parapet / attic / cresting) must span the
  facade's full width and have real height — a deliberate culmination of the body, never a
  small cap perched on a wide base.
