## Finalized design document
# The Gate of the Sunwardens — Temple Facade

## 1. Lore & Setting
Raised by the Sunwarden order of a salt-road empire to commemorate a thousand-day caravan crossing, this is a triumphal temple-gate: a victory monument fused with a shrine. Pilgrims pass beneath its vault to "walk through the sun." Like the reference, it is a monument of civic memory, so its walls are *carved stories*, not blank surface.

## 2. Aesthetic & Architectural Logic
From the photograph: a monolithic, nearly square slab pierced by one colossal round arch, flanked by two stout piers, crowned by a heavy, layered attic. Its power comes from **mass, a single dominant opening, and bands stacked in strict horizontal hierarchy** (plinth → piers → entablature → frieze → attic). We keep that logic: symmetry, one void, deep relief on flat fields, and horizontal bands that tame a tall mass.

## 3. Color Palette — *Split-complementary (terracotta vs. teal-blue, with gold)*
- **Dominant — `terracotta` (glazed or plain)**: the great piers and wall slabs; warm earth reads as monumental mass.
- **Supporting — `warped_planks` / `prismarine_bricks` (teal)**: the arch voussoirs, panel fields, and attic frieze; the cool complement makes the opening the focal point.
- **Accent — `gold_block`**: cornice lines, panel borders, and keystone; small, bright, and precious.
- **Shadow/neutral — `blackstone`**: the plinth and the arch's deep soffit, grounding the warmth and deepening recess.

Warm masses with a cool, gilded ring around the void create contrast of temperature and draw the eye straight to the entrance.

## 4. Motifs & Ornament
- **Sunburst coffers**: in the reference, the arch soffit has recessed square coffers; we use alternating gold/teal coffer cells in the vault.
- **Relief panels**: the reference's four sculptural groups become four recessed teal panels (two on the piers, two above the arch spandrels) with gold frames and a gold block "figure" pattern pushed forward one block.
- **Keystone sun**: a gold block at the arch crown, flanked by stair "rays."
- **Frieze of soldiers**: a continuous band of alternating teal/gold blocks beneath the attic, echoing the reference's carved procession.

## 5. Features & Proportion
- **Overall**: 33 wide × 36 tall, relief depth 4. Ratio roughly 1:1.1, as in the reference.
- **Plinth**: 3 high, blackstone, projecting 1 block.
- **Piers**: each 9 wide, terracotta, rising to y=24; central opening 15 wide.
- **Arch**: round vault, 15 wide × 20 tall (opening ≈ 55% of the height), springing at y=12; stair-stepped voussoir ring 2 blocks thick in teal, recessed 3 deep.
- **Secondary openings**: small arched niches (3 × 7) in the pier sides.
- **Entablature**: a 2-high gold-lined cornice at y=24–26 projecting 2 blocks via stairs/slabs.
- **Attic**: 7 high (y=27–34), terracotta with a row of teal panel insets (about 20% of the total height), capped by a stepped gold-trimmed crenellation.
- **Relief layering**: three depths — plinth/cornice proud (+2), walls flush (0), panels and coffers recessed (−1 to −3).

## Scale & RELIEF — use it (this is judged)
- Width up to ~56 (X), height up to ~48 (Y) including the crown. Build big.
- DEEP relief: up to ~24 blocks of depth into −Z, and genuinely use it. Columns/buttresses
  project several blocks PROUD of the wall; the central portal RECESSES several blocks deep;
  cornices, string courses, and the crown step forward and back across MULTIPLE Z-layers. A
  near-flat screen only 4–6 deep is a failure.
- CROWN proportion: the crowning element (pediment / parapet / attic / cresting) must span the
  facade's full width and have real height — a deliberate culmination of the body, never a
  small cap perched on a wide base.
