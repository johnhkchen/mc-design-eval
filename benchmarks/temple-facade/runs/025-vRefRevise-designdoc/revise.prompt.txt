# Temple of the Jade Meridian

## 1. Lore & setting
A tide-temple of the Asravi, a river people who borrowed the Mughal imperial grammar but turned its funerary grief into living devotion — the white tomb's stillness reforged as a shrine to the green dawn over water. Same proportion, opposite mood: not mourning, but renewal.

## 2. Aesthetic & architectural logic
The reference gives perfect bilateral symmetry and a tripartite front: a tall central **pīshtāq** (recessed pointed-arch iwan) flanked by two stacked registers of smaller arched niches, the whole crowned by a bulbous onion dome on a high drum and pinned by four corner minarets. The iwan's deep shadow does the heavy lifting — relief, not paint, carries the drama. I keep that hierarchy: one commanding void, framed silence around it.

## 3. Color palette (split-complementary)
Jade-green dominant against its split-complements, amber-gold and vermilion.
- **prismarine_bricks** — dominant body (~60%), the jade field.
- **dark_prismarine** — recess and iwan interior (~15%), deepening the shadow the reference earns with depth.
- **gold_block** — string-courses, dome, finials (~12%), the amber accent.
- **red_nether_bricks** — calligraphic borders and rosettes (~8%), vermilion punctuation.
- **smooth_quartz** — plinth and crisp arch trim (~5%), to frame, never to fill.

The pale marble of the reference becomes information only; the temple commits to bold green.

## 4. Motifs & ornament
Gold ogee frames around every niche; a continuous gold string-course banding base, drum, and minarets into one read. The reference's calligraphic pīshtāq border becomes a vermilion fret. Lotus-petal collar in gold at the dome's base; four small chattri kiosks shouldering the central dome.

## 5. Architectural features & proportion
- **Base:** quartz plinth, 1 part of an 8-part facade height.
- **Body:** jade cube, the central iwan recessed 5 blocks deep, its arch rising to ⅔ of the cube.
- **Flanks:** two-over-two niche registers, each half the iwan's width.
- **Entrance:** pointed-arch portal inside the iwan, gold-framed, ~1:1.6 portal-to-body.
- **Crown:** onion dome whose belly **overhangs the drum** (never a pyramid), height ≈ ⅓ of the total; gold finial spike.
- **Minarets:** four engaged corner shafts at full facade height, gold-ringed, balconied chattris atop.

## A facade is ONE connected plane (critical)
Borrow the reference's RHYTHM, not its 3-D standalone parts. A real building's corner towers or
minarets are freestanding in the round; in a flat elevation they must read as ENGAGED — bonded to
the wall and projecting forward as relief, NEVER as detached pillars with sky between them. Do not
pull columns, towers, or framing off the body; every mass stays connected into a single facade.
